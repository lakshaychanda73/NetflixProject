02

Three things stop the line

No client conversation until these clear. Each one is a personal liability, not a developer problem.

RERA registration

No registration was traceable in the public record at the time of writing. No number means no advertising, no marketing, no booking. The penalty under s.62 is ₹10,000 per day, capped at 5% of the property's cost — about ₹30.8 lakh on a ₹6.16 Cr flat.

Area definition

Until “₹55,000 per sq ft” names its denominator in writing, it is not a price. Carpet versus saleable swings the only comparable metric a buyer has by up to 45%. The briefing indicates carpet — get it on paper.

Unit & tower count

The pitch is “boutique, 250 homes, 3 towers”. The public record from the JV announcement says 321 homes across 6 towers of 20 storeys. Exclusivity is the whole argument. If the filing says 321, the pitch is wrong — and you will be the one who said it.

Inside the hoarding

Four things a site visit establishes that no brochure can — and two traps the photographs themselves set for you.

What the evidence supports

The sales gallery is real, and it is steel. Metal-deck floors, columns painted, slabs cast, first-fix electrical run. Steel because steel is fast.✓
Trees are being protected, not cleared. Mature trees wrapped in netting with cast concrete collars — textbook tree protection, and a deliberate cost.✓
Infrastructure is going in first. Concreted internal roads with kerbs and paver edges, drainage rings and a formal site office, all before the towers rise.✓
The water really is at the plot edge. One frame has raw cut rock in the foreground and the water body immediately beyond it.✓

Concreted roads and protected trees before a single tower is the opposite of how an undercapitalised developer sequences a site. It is also the most persuasive thing you can photograph — a competitor selling a ₹33,000 Chandivali flat has nothing like it. Walk clients past the wrapped trees.

What the evidence warns

The neighbours are close, on more than one side. A tall tower sits directly over the boundary hoarding; informal low-rise housing abuts the works elsewhere.⚠
Clearance activity is under way adjacent. An excavator working through rubble amid informal structures in the middle ground. Ask what that land is and what is planned for it.⚠
Rock excavation is live and large. Blasted basalt in volume, next to occupied housing — complaint, stop-work and night-work-restriction risk.⚠

None of this is disqualifying. All of it is better heard from you, on the site visit, than discovered alone on a second visit without you.

Trap one · which lake is it?

The sales gallery is a two-to-three level structure. The developer's own banding puts a partial Powai Lake view above the 10th floor and a clear one above the 13th, because a building sits in between. So anything shot from the gallery is the south outlook — the internal lake, not Powai Lake. The best water frames in hand are almost certainly internal-lake frames. They are still good; they are just not what a buyer will assume. Re-shoot every view frame with the floor and a compass bearing noted. Showing an internal-lake photograph while saying “Powai Lake” is a misrepresentation you made, and it surfaces on handover day in 2030.

Trap two · which season is it?

Every frame in hand is a September frame — end of monsoon, water high, banks lush, canopy full. Even within one visit the water reads differently: clear olive-green in one frame, turbid brown at the excavation edge in another. The same water body in April or May is a different photograph. Re-shoot in March–April and own the dry-season truth as well as the monsoon one. A buyer who sees only the September frame and moves in during May will remember exactly who showed it to them.

Where ₹55,000 actually sits

Carpet basis where stated. The ask is above everything this micro-market has printed for new product — and below both the best Hiranandani resale stock and what a lake view already fetches next door.

The number that matters most. Channel partners in the briefing said the price “should be ₹45–48”. The developer conceded that a “normal building” on that plot would be ₹45. So the gap you are being asked to sell is 15–20% above what the channel itself believes — and the only thing that closes it is the specification argument below.

The view ladder

The developer's own floor-by-floor banding. This is the most commercially sensitive material in the dossier, and the part most likely to be repeated carelessly.

FL 13–17
Clear Powai Lake view. The premium band. The 4 BHK XL sits here. Expect the highest ₹/sq ft.
FL 11–12
Partial Powai Lake view. The value sweet spot — ask for the price step up to 13.
FL 2–10
Amenity deck, podium green, city skyline or internal lake. Lower floor rise, faster absorption. Internal-lake units here may be under-priced at launch.

Why the band starts at 10

Because there is a building between this plot and Powai Lake. The briefing said so directly. Below the 10th floor there is no Powai Lake view at all. Orientation is north to Powai Lake, south to the internal lake, east to the Hiranandani skyline, west to Andheri. Get the intervening building's height and owner, and whether it can be redeveloped taller — then get the unit-wise view schedule in writing. And never promise permanence: nothing in Indian law protects a sightline, and there is another tower going up next door.

The design team

ArchitectureKapadia Associates, Mumbai

A local architect was chosen deliberately: only someone living in Mumbai designs for a five-month monsoon, the summer heat — and for Vastu. The field note reading “Vastu complaint” is the opposite of a problem; Vastu compliance is a stated design driver.

The monsoon question — the one that separates you from everyone else in the room. A 160 sq ft open deck at floor 15 is not a balcony; it is a small cantilevered roof over someone's living room. The answer given: the deck above shades the one below, but was deliberately not extended further because a deeper overhang would darken the living room. Daylight was chosen over full rain cover. The deck is floored in wood-look tile and run with a slot drain. Now ask for the threshold section detail — slot drain, upstand, membrane upturn, drip edge, fall. A developer who has it will respect you. One who doesn't has just told you something.

Township scale, boutique density

The entire positioning is a deliberate refusal of the township model. The developer's own words: in a township the amenities are built for 2,000 apartments — and among those 2,000 you have a one-bed and a four-bed using the same clubhouse. A 380 sq ft carpet resident and an 1,100 sq ft carpet resident queueing for the same court is the mismatch they are selling against.

The four you personally control

1. Don't market without two registration numbers — yours and the project's. 2. Only ever quote RERA carpet, in writing, with the balcony stated separately. 3. Never promise a view — there is a building between this plot and Powai Lake. 4. Never project a return — not the Gurugram 3×, not a CAGR, not a resale price. Give the mechanism and the history, and let them do the arithmetic.

The pre-launch marketing trap. The developer asked partners not to use the Hines name before RERA — sincere advice that protects their brand and their partners' ad accounts. It does not protect you. S.3 of RERA prohibits advertising an unregistered project whether or not you name the developer; an anonymised ad with no RERA number and no QR code is more exposed, not less. Until both numbers exist, run micro-market content that names no project. You will have built the list by the time the filing lands.

The first ninety days

Nothing in this dossier matters until the licence is in hand. Six to ten weeks, end to end. Start Monday.

Weeks 1–2

Licence & foundations

  • Register as an individual (₹10,000), not a firm (₹1,00,000) — convert once volume is proven
  • Enrol in the 20-hour MahaRERA training (~₹5,900)
  • Book the competency exam (₹1,062)
  • Bank account, CA, GST assessment
  • Build the referral bench: one advocate, one CA, two bankers
  • Zero project marketing. Not one post.
Weeks 2–4

Diligence

  • Send the 45 questions as one email
  • Shoot the real-footage library — the wrapped trees and concreted roads, and the downside frames for your own file
  • Diarise a March–April re-shoot of every water frame
  • Walk and time the 50 m from the CCD
  • Visit the 16,000 sq ft sales gallery when it opens
  • Visit three comps in person and collect their price sheets
  • Check the MahaRERA portal daily
Weeks 4–8

Build the machine

  • Sit the exam; file the registration the day the certificates land
  • Credential one-pager, comp pack, cost-sheet calculator
  • Learn the objection library until it's conversational, not recited
  • Start micro-market content — lawful, project-free: what Metro 3 did to Powai; carpet versus saleable; township versus boutique
Weeks 8–12

Go live

  • Gate check: your agent number ☐   project RERA number ☐ — if either box is empty, do not proceed
  • Run every asset through the advertising pre-flight
  • Launch to the list you already built
  • Target: 40 qualified conversations, 12 site visits, 2 closings
  • Start the quarterly progress-report ritual on day one
Your economics. At an ₹8.76 Cr average agreement value, the gap between a 1.0% and a 2.0% payout slab over eight units is ₹70 lakh for the same work. That negotiation is the highest-paid hour of your year. Your leverage: the developer conceded that Hines is a new brand in Mumbai and that partners must build it — that is unpaid brand-building labour, and it is worth a higher slab, marketing co-funding, or segment exclusivity. Ask.

Your edge is not access. It is honesty. Thirty other partners will have the same renders and the same floor plates. You will be the one who says: there is a building between us and Powai Lake, the yield is 1.8%, plan for 2031, and here is the settlement on the south-west boundary. In a ₹7 crore sale to someone who prices risk for a living, that is not a handicap. It is the entire business.
