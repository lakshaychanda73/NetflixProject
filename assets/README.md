# Field evidence

Photographs taken on site. Read against **Master Report 3.6**. These are the most valuable
assets in this pack — every competing agent has the render; almost none of them has been
inside the hoarding.

## The lake frames

| File | What it shows |
|---|---|
| `site-01-lake-view.jpg` | Elevated view across a water body toward a wall of 17–25 storey towers, one with a green dome, a rocky forested hillock behind, fully vegetated banks on both sides |
| `site-02-lake-view.jpg` | Wider frame of the same water body, more towers including curved balcony stacks |
| `site-05-gallery-upper-outlook.jpg` | Shot from the **upper deck of the sales gallery**: dark-green water at lower left, a deep unbroken tree canopy across the middle, then a mid-rise skyline, a white mosque with minaret at left-centre, and a built-up hillock on the right horizon |

**What they prove.** A genuine water-and-green outlook exists, from a floor you can stand on today.

**What they do not prove.** That any particular home gets it. **And they are almost certainly the
*internal* lake, not Powai Lake** — the sales gallery is a two-to-three level structure, and the
developer's own banding puts partial Powai Lake views above the 10th floor and clear views above the
13th, because a building sits in between. Anything shot from the gallery is the south outlook.

> **Before you show these to anyone:** re-shoot each frame with a **compass bearing and the floor
> noted**. If you present an internal-lake frame while saying "Powai Lake", that is a
> misrepresentation you personally made, and it surfaces in 2030 when the buyer moves in.

**The seasonal trap.** These are September frames, at the end of the monsoon. Water level is high,
banks are lush, canopy is full. The same water body in April or May is a different photograph
entirely — lower, browner, thinner. Compare `site-01` (clear olive-green, full banks) with the
turbid brown water at the excavation edge in `site-04`. **Shoot the same frames again in
March–April before you build a campaign on them.**

## What the works frames establish

**1. The sales gallery is real, and it is steel.** `site-04` and `site-06` show a steel-framed,
metal-deck structure at finishing stage — scaffold up, columns painted, slabs cast, first-fix
electrical run. Steel because it is fast. This corroborates "structure complete, finishing work,
opening within a month", and it means the 16,000 sq ft gallery is a near-term certainty rather than
a promise.

**2. Trees are being protected, not cleared.** `site-07` shows textbook tree protection — trunk
wrapping and a concrete collar. That is a deliberate, auditable cost, and it is the strongest
physical evidence yet that the "forest" is a retained asset rather than a render. **Ask how many
trees are being retained versus transplanted versus felled, and ask for the tree-cutting permission.**

**3. Site infrastructure is going in first.** Concrete roads, kerbs, drainage rings and a formal site
office before the towers. That is institutional practice and it is worth pointing out — it is the
opposite of how an undercapitalised developer sequences a site.

**4. Rock excavation is live and large.** Blasted basalt in volume across `site-03`, `site-04` and
`site-07`. Two basements in Powai basalt means controlled blasting or rock-breaking, next to
occupied housing. **Schedule risk, complaint risk, and night-work restriction risk. Ask what the
excavation methodology is and whether any complaints or stop-work notices are on record.**

**5. The neighbours are close, on more than one side.** `site-06` puts a tall cream tower directly
over the boundary hoarding. `site-03` and `site-04` put informal low-rise housing hard against the
works. `site-05` shows an orange excavator working through rubble amid informal structures in the
middle ground — clearance activity adjacent to the plot. **Ask what that land is and what its
long-term plan is.** An SRA scheme next door is both a risk — years of construction and dust — and
an upside, since it eventually replaces a low-rise edge with a planned one.

## Re-shoot protocol

Per Master Report 7C.2, and now with three additions from these frames:

1. Timestamp and geotag everything.
2. **Record the floor and compass bearing of every view frame.** Non-negotiable after the
   internal-lake-versus-Powai-Lake question above.
3. **Re-shoot the water frames in March–April**, so you own the dry-season truth as well as the
   monsoon one.
4. Shoot the 50 m walk from the CCD as one continuous take, and the DP Road No. 9 dead end.
5. Shoot the downside deliberately — the settlement edge, the neighbouring tower, the rock works —
   for your own file. You need to answer "what's on the other side?" the first time it is asked.
6. Re-shoot everything from the mock-up flat the day it opens.
7. No AI upscaling, no sky replacement, no "artist's impression" framing. The developer's own point
   stands: real footage now outperforms AI.
8. **Hold all of it until the project's RERA number exists.** Then it goes out on day one.
