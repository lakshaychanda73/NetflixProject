## 2. The Mumbai Metropolitan Region

**MMR** covers roughly 6,300 sq km and includes Greater Mumbai plus Thane, Navi Mumbai, Kalyan-
Dombivli, Mira-Bhayandar, Vasai-Virar, Panvel, Bhiwandi and Ulhasnagar. It is planned at the
regional level by **MMRDA**, while Greater Mumbai's building rules come from **MCGM/BMC** under
**DCPR 2034**, Navi Mumbai from **CIDCO**, and slum redevelopment from the **SRA**.

**Knowing which authority governs a plot tells you which rulebook applies** — and that is the first
question a serious buyer or investor will ask you.

### 2.1 The micro-markets

| Zone | Areas | Character |
|---|---|---|
| **South Mumbai (SoBo)** | Colaba, Cuffe Parade, Malabar Hill, Altamount/Carmichael Rd, Worli, Prabhadevi | Highest values in India. Supply almost entirely **redevelopment** of cessed buildings. Limited land, deep legacy title complexity |
| **Central Mumbai** | Lower Parel, Dadar, Mahalaxmi, Byculla, Wadala, Sewri | The mill-land transformation. Grade-A offices + luxury residential. **Atal Setu / Sewri** has reframed the eastern edge |
| **Western suburbs** | Bandra, Khar, Santacruz, Andheri, Goregaon, Malad, Kandivali, Borivali, Dahisar | The deepest residential market. BKC anchors commercial. Metro Line 2A/7 along the Western corridor |
| **Eastern suburbs** | Powai, Chandivali, Vikhroli, Ghatkopar, Mulund, Bhandup, Kanjurmarg | Strong end-user demand, better value per sq ft, LBS/Eastern Express corridors |
| **Thane** | Thane city, Ghodbunder Rd, Majiwada, Kolshet | Large-format supply, big launch volumes, metro and road-led growth |
| **Navi Mumbai** | Vashi, Nerul, Belapur, Kharghar, Panvel, Ulwe, Taloja | **The structural growth story** — airport, Atal Setu, metro. Leading MMR new-supply share |
| **Extended MMR** | Kalyan-Dombivli, Mira-Bhayandar, Vasai-Virar, Bhiwandi | Affordable/mid-segment volume; Bhiwandi is India's largest warehousing hub |

### 2.2 The infrastructure that is repricing the map ⏱

| Project | What it changes |
|---|---|
| **Mumbai Trans Harbour Link (Atal Setu)** | Sewri ↔ Nhava Sheva. Collapses the travel time to Navi Mumbai; the single biggest driver of the Navi Mumbai/Ulwe/Panvel story |
| **Navi Mumbai International Airport** | A second airport; reframes Panvel/Ulwe/Kharghar as an airport catchment |
| **Metro network** (2A, 7, 1, 3 and expanding) | Line 3 (Colaba–Bandra–SEEPZ) links SoBo to the western business districts underground |
| **Coastal Road** | South–west corridor travel times |
| **Eastern Freeway, SCLR, Goregaon-Mulund Link Road** | East–west connectivity, historically Mumbai's weakest axis |

💼 **How infrastructure actually prices in:** value tends to move on **announcement**, again on
**visible construction**, and again on **commissioning** — and the last move is often the smallest,
because the first two already anticipated it. An agent who tells a client "prices will jump when
the metro opens" is usually about three years late.

## 3. FSI, TDR and land economics 🔴

This is the section that separates people who understand Mumbai from people who just live here.

### 3.1 FSI (Floor Space Index)
```
FSI = Total permissible built-up area ÷ Plot area
```
A 1,000 sq m plot with FSI 3.0 permits 3,000 sq m of built-up area.

**Under DCPR 2034, permissible FSI is linked to road width** — taller buildings only where the road
can carry the traffic. The regulations sort residential and commercial plots into **five road-width
bands**, each carrying a fixed quantum of **premium FSI** and **TDR** on top of the basic zonal FSI.
Indicatively, permissible FSI runs from about **1.00** (a suburban plot on a road under 9 m) to
**3.00** (an Island City plot on a road of 27 m or more) — **before** any 33-series incentive is added.

⏱ **Verify current bands and quanta in DCPR 2034 itself** — they are amended periodically.

### 3.2 TDR (Transferable Development Rights)
When a landowner's plot is acquired for a public purpose — a road, a school, a garden — they may be
compensated not in cash but in **development rights usable elsewhere**. TDR is bought, sold and
"loaded" onto a receiving plot within the rules.

**Why you care:** TDR is a **traded commodity with a price**. Its cost is a real input into a
developer's feasibility, and when TDR prices move, project economics move with them.

### 3.3 The 33-series — Mumbai's redevelopment engine

Mumbai builds mostly by **rebuilding**. The 33-series of DCPR 2034 grants incentive FSI to make
that viable.

| Regulation | Applies to | Key features |
|---|---|---|
| **33(7), 33(7)(A), 33(7)(B)** | **Cessed buildings** in the Island City existing prior to **30 September 1969** | Total FSI of **3.00** of gross plot area, or rehab + incentive, whichever is more. Every residential occupier guaranteed a minimum of **27.88 sq m (300 sq ft) carpet**, up to a ceiling of **120 sq m** |
| **33(9)** | **Cluster redevelopment** — Urban Renewal Cluster Development Scheme | Minimum **4,000 sq m** in the Island City, **6,000 sq m** in the suburbs. Incentive FSI of **55%** of the rehabilitation area |
| **33(10)** | **Slum redevelopment (SRA)** | Free rehabilitation tenements for eligible slum dwellers, funded by sale-component FSI. Buildings eligible under other 33-series provisions receive the **higher** of their own benefit or the 33(10) benefit |
| **33(5), 33(20), 33(24)** | Other categories — MHADA layouts, IT, commercial | Category-specific incentives |

⏱ These regulations are amended regularly, including via a revamped cluster policy for Mumbai.
**Always check the current DCPR text and the latest UDD notifications before relying on a number.**

### 3.4 The economics of a redevelopment 💼

A society redevelopment has a simple logic:

```
Total buildable area  =  REHAB component (free, to existing members)
                       + SALE component (the developer's revenue)

Developer's economics: SALE revenue − (rehab construction + sale construction
                       + rent/corpus to members + premiums + TDR + finance) = margin
```

**What this means for you as an agent:**
- The **sale component** is the inventory you'll be selling.
- The project is only viable if incentive FSI is enough. **When a developer says "the numbers don't
  work", they mean the sale component is too small relative to the rehab obligation.**
- Members receiving a **corpus and rent** during construction is normal.
- 🚩 The chronic risk: societies signing with a developer who cannot fund the rehab, leaving members
  out of their homes and out of rent. Mumbai has many of these. **When you sell in a redevelopment,
  check who the society is and whether the members are being paid on time** — that is a live
  indicator of the developer's cash position.

## 4. The market, in numbers ⏱

Snapshot at the time of writing. **These change every quarter — always cite the latest report.**

| Metric | Latest reading | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Mumbai housing sales, H1 2026 | **47,355 units** — ~**28%** of all homes sold across India's top 8 cities | Knight Frank |
| India new launches, H1 2026 | **187,350 units**, +4% YoY | Knight Frank |
| India unsold inventory, H1 2026 | **525,695 units**, +4% YoY | Knight Frank |
| Quarters-to-sell | **6** — described as a healthy level | Knight Frank |
| Mumbai new launches, Q1 2026 | **15,823 units**; Navi Mumbai **30.3%**, Thane **16.7%**, South Central **12.9%** | JLL |
| MMR new launches, Q2 2026 | **34,550 units** — roughly **one-third** of all new supply across the top 7 cities | ANAROCK |
| All-India House Price Index, Q2 2025-26 | **+2.2% YoY** (moderating from ~7%), new base 2022-23 | RBI |

**How to read those together:** Mumbai is India's largest residential market by sales, but
**inventory is accumulating nationally** and increasingly concentrated in the **premium and luxury**
segments while lower-priced housing keeps absorbing. Growth within MMR has shifted decisively
**away from the traditional corridors** towards Navi Mumbai and Thane, driven by the airport, Atal
Setu, metro and coastal road. Meanwhile the RBI's house price index shows price growth
**moderating sharply** — volume is holding up better than price.

That paragraph, updated each quarter, is a genuinely useful thing to be able to say out loud.

## 9. Case study 📋 — the Navi Mumbai question

> A client has ₹1.6 crore. They can buy a **compact 2BHK in Chembur** (established, ready,
> immediate rental) or a **larger 3BHK in Ulwe** (under construction, near the airport and Atal Setu).
> They ask: *"Which will appreciate more?"*

**A professional answer is not a prediction. It's a structure:**

**Ulwe — the case for.** The infrastructure thesis is real and unusually concrete: an operating
airport, Atal Setu, metro. Navi Mumbai leads MMR new-supply share. Lower entry price means a given
absolute price rise is a larger percentage return.

**Ulwe — the case against.** *Supply is the risk.* Growth corridors attract developers, and MMR is
producing tens of thousands of new units a quarter with **national unsold inventory rising**. In a
corridor with abundant developable land, new supply can absorb demand growth and cap price growth
for years. **Rental demand also lags physical infrastructure**, often by several years — so the
early years may deliver poor yield and no appreciation simultaneously. Plus: completion risk.

**Chembur — the case for.** Established micro-market, constrained supply, existing rental demand
from day one, no construction risk, immediately mortgageable and immediately sellable. **Liquidity
is a return.** The eastern suburbs have benefited from the Eastern Freeway and SCLR.

**Chembur — the case against.** Lower percentage upside; already priced for its advantages.

**What you actually tell them — three questions, not an answer:**
1. **What's the holding period?** Under 5 years → established market. 10+ years → the growth
   corridor thesis has time to work.
2. **Do they need income now?** Chembur rents from month one. Ulwe rents in ~3 years, at a lower yield.
3. **Can they carry both EMI and rent through an 18-month delay, plus a possible further delay?**
   If not, under-construction is not suitable regardless of the thesis.

**And be honest about the limit of your knowledge:** you can describe supply, infrastructure and
absorption. You cannot forecast prices, and any agent who says they can is selling, not advising.
Saying so out loud is what makes the rest of your advice credible.

## 10. Quiz — 10 questions

1. Which authority governs building approvals in Greater Mumbai? Navi Mumbai? Slum redevelopment?
2. Define FSI. What is permissible FSI linked to under DCPR 2034?
3. What is TDR, and why does its price matter to a developer?
4. Which regulation covers cessed buildings, and what's the cut-off date?
5. Minimum carpet area guaranteed to a residential occupier under 33(7)?
6. Minimum plot area for a cluster scheme in the Island City vs the suburbs?
7. Incentive FSI under 33(9) as a percentage of the rehabilitation area?
8. What are "rehab component" and "sale component"?
9. Which MMR sub-market led new launches in Q1 2026, and with what share?
10. What does "quarters-to-sell" measure?

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. **MCGM/BMC** (Greater Mumbai, under DCPR 2034) · **CIDCO** (Navi Mumbai) · **SRA** (slum redevelopment). **MMRDA** plans at the regional level.
2. FSI = total permissible built-up area ÷ plot area. Under DCPR 2034 it is linked to **road width**, via five road-width bands.
3. Development rights granted as compensation where land is taken for a public purpose, usable/loadable elsewhere. It is **traded at a price**, so it is a direct input cost in a developer's feasibility.
4. **33(7)** (with 33(7)(A) and (B)) — cessed buildings in the Island City existing prior to **30 September 1969**.
5. **27.88 sq m (300 sq ft) carpet**, up to a ceiling of 120 sq m.
6. **4,000 sq m** Island City; **6,000 sq m** suburbs and extended suburbs.
7. **55%** of the rehabilitation area.
8. **Rehab** = area given free to existing occupants/members. **Sale** = the area the developer sells, which funds the whole scheme.
9. **Navi Mumbai**, with **30.3%** of Mumbai's Q1 2026 new launches (JLL).
10. How many quarters it would take to sell existing unsold inventory at the current sales pace — an inventory-overhang measure.
