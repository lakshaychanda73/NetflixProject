> **Official syllabus:** MATP Module 2 — 2.1 Preamble, Purpose and Objectives · 2.2 Sections of the Act · 2.3 Key Components.

## 1. What I need to learn

Not the whole Act. **Roughly 25 sections** carry almost all the exam value and all the
day-to-day agent value. This module gives you those, in the order that makes them make sense.

The mental model: the Act does four things.
1. **Forces disclosure** (register the project, publish the details).
2. **Controls the money** (70% in a separate account, 10% cap before agreement).
3. **Fixes accountability** (promoter duties, agent duties, allottee duties).
4. **Gives a fast remedy** (a regulator and a tribunal instead of civil courts).

Everything else is machinery.

## 2. Simple explanation

### 2.1 Pre-RERA: why this law exists

Before 2016, an Indian homebuyer's position was roughly this:

- The builder wrote the agreement. It was one-sided, often unregistered, and the buyer signed it because the alternative was not buying.
- **Area was sold in "super built-up"** — a number the builder defined. Two flats sold as "1,000 sq ft" could differ by 200 sq ft of actual usable space.
- Money from Project A funded land for Project B. When B failed, A stalled. Buyers paying EMIs on a flat that did not exist.
- Delay had no price. The agreement gave the builder 6 months' grace, then 12 more, then "force majeure".
- The remedy was a civil suit or a consumer forum. Both took years.
- Brokers were entirely unregulated.

RERA is a direct answer to each of those. When you're studying a section, ask *"which pre-RERA
abuse is this killing?"* — that's usually the fastest way to remember it.

### 2.2 Preamble, purpose and objectives (syllabus 2.1)

The Act's own preamble says it is to establish the Authority for **regulation and promotion** of
the sector, to ensure **sale of plot/apartment/building in an efficient and transparent manner**,
to **protect the interest of consumers**, to establish an **adjudicating mechanism for speedy
dispute redressal**, and to establish an **Appellate Tribunal**.

Note the ordering — regulation *and promotion*. RERA is not purely a consumer statute; it is also
meant to bring institutional capital into a sector that was too opaque to invest in.

**Statutory milestones:**

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| 10 & 15 March 2016 | Passed by Rajya Sabha and Lok Sabha |
| **25 March 2016** | Received assent — **Act No. 16 of 2016** |
| **1 May 2016** | 61 of 92 sections brought into force |
| **1 May 2017** | Remaining sections in force; projects had to register |

| III | 11–18 | **Functions and duties of promoter** |

| V | 20–40 | The Real Estate **Regulatory Authority** |

| VIII | 59–72 | **Offences, penalties and adjudication** |
| IX | 73–78 | Finance, accounts, audit |

### 2.4 Key components (syllabus 2.3) — the six that matter

**① Project registration (s.3).** No advertising, marketing, booking, selling or offering for sale
without registration, in any planning area, for projects above the threshold. The **threshold**
(s.3(2)(a)): land **over 500 sq m** *or* **more than 8 apartments**, inclusive of all phases.
Also exempt: projects where completion certificate was obtained before commencement of the Act,
and renovation/repair not involving marketing or new allotment.
👉 **Each phase is a standalone project** and registers separately.

**② The 70% rule (s.4(2)(l)(D)).** Seventy per cent of amounts realised from allottees goes into a
**separate account in a scheduled bank**, to cover land and construction cost only, withdrawn in
proportion to completion and certified by Architect, Engineer and CA. This is the single provision
that killed cross-project fund diversion.

**③ The 10% rule (s.13(1)).** A promoter shall **not accept more than 10%** of the cost of the
apartment/plot/building as an advance or application fee **without first entering into a written
Agreement for Sale and registering it**.

**④ Carpet area (s.2(k)).** The **net usable floor area** of an apartment, **excluding** external
walls, service shafts, exclusive balcony/verandah and exclusive open terrace, but **including**
internal partition walls. Sale must be on carpet area. This ended super-built-up selling.

**⑤ Interest is symmetrical (s.18, s.19(7)).** If the promoter delays, they pay interest to the
allottee; if the allottee delays payment, they pay interest to the promoter — **at the same rate**
prescribed by state rules. This symmetry is a favourite exam point.

**⑥ Defect liability — 5 years (s.14(3)).** Any structural defect or defect in workmanship,
quality or provision of services brought to the promoter's notice **within 5 years of possession**
must be rectified **within 30 days** without further charge; failing which, the allottee is
entitled to compensation.

## 3. 🔴 RERA Sections Every Real Estate Agent Should Know

This is the table to learn. If you memorise one thing in this course, make it this.

| Section | Topic | Simple meaning | Why an agent needs it | Exam importance |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **2(d)** | Allottee | Buyer of plot/apartment, incl. subsequent purchaser; excludes tenant | Defines who your client legally is | 🔴 High |
| **2(k)** | Carpet area | Net usable floor area; excl. external walls/shafts/balcony/terrace, incl. internal partitions | You must quote carpet, never super built-up | 🔴 **Very high** |
| **2(zk)** | Promoter | Builder, development authority, **JDA landowner** | Tells your client who is liable | 🔴 High |
| **2(zm)** | Real estate agent | Negotiates/acts for another in transfer, for fee | **Your own legal definition** | 🔴 **Very high** |
| **2(zn)** | Real estate project | Development of building/apartments/plots for sale | What triggers registration | 🟠 Medium |
| **3** | Prior registration of project | No advertising/marketing/booking/selling without registration. Threshold: >500 sq m or >8 apartments | You cannot market an unregistered project — s.10 forbids it | 🔴 **Very high** |
| **4** | Application for registration | What the promoter must disclose + **70% separate account** | Everything you verify in due diligence comes from here | 🔴 **Very high** |
| **5** | Grant of registration | Authority grants/rejects within 30 days; deemed registration if silent | Explains "registration in process" claims | 🟠 Medium |
| **6** | Extension | Extension for force majeure; ordinarily **up to 1 year** | Repeated extensions are a red flag | 🔴 High |
| **7** | Revocation | Authority may revoke for default/unfair practice/violation | What happens to buyers in a revoked project | 🟠 Medium |
| **8** | Consequences of lapse/revocation | Authority may allow association of allottees to complete | The buyer's fallback | 🟢 Lower |
| **9** | **Registration of agents** | No agent shall facilitate sale in a registered project without registration | **This is your licence** | 🔴 **Very high** |
| **10** | **Functions of agents** | Don't facilitate unregistered projects; maintain books; don't make false statements; assist allottee to obtain documents | **Your job description in law** | 🔴 **Very high** |
| **11** | Functions & duties of promoter | Website disclosures, QPR, sanctioned plans, association formation, conveyance | What you check on the portal | 🔴 High |
| **12** | Veracity of advertisement | If allottee relied on a false advert and paid, they may **withdraw with full refund + interest** | You often *are* the channel for the advert | 🔴 **Very high** |
| **13** | Agreement for sale | **10% cap** before registered AFS | The most commonly broken rule in practice | 🔴 **Very high** |
| **14** | Adherence to sanctioned plans | No change without consent; **2/3rds of allottees** for other alterations; **5-year defect liability** | Post-possession complaints | 🔴 High |
| **15** | Transfer of project to third party | Needs 2/3rds allottees + Authority approval | Who is liable after a takeover | 🟢 Lower |
| **16** | Insurance | Promoter to insure title and construction | 🟢 Lower | 🟢 Lower |
| **17** | Transfer of title / conveyance | Conveyance to association within specified period | Deemed conveyance disputes | 🟠 Medium |
| **18** | **Return of amount and compensation** | Failure to complete/give possession → refund **with interest** + compensation, or interest for every month of delay if allottee stays | **The buyer's main weapon** | 🔴 **Very high** |
| **19** | Rights & duties of allottee | Right to information, possession, refund; duty to pay, take possession within **2 months of OC**, join the association | Your client's obligations too | 🔴 **Very high** |
| **31** | Filing complaints | Any aggrieved person may complain to the Authority or Adjudicating Officer | How your client gets relief | 🔴 High |
| **34** | Functions of the Authority | Register & regulate, maintain the website database, redress | What MahaRERA is *for* | 🟠 Medium |
| **40** | Recovery | Dues recoverable as **arrears of land revenue** | Why RERA orders have teeth | 🟠 Medium |
| **43(5)** | Appeal | Appeal to Appellate Tribunal within **60 days**; promoter must pre-deposit ≥30% of penalty/amount | The next step after an order | 🔴 High |
| **59** | Non-registration penalty (promoter) | Up to **10%** of estimated project cost; continued violation → imprisonment up to **3 years** and/or further fine up to 10% | Why registration is not optional | 🔴 High |
| **61** | Contravention of other provisions | Up to **5%** of estimated project cost | General promoter penalty | 🟠 Medium |
| **62** | **Penalty on agents (s.9/s.10)** | **₹10,000 per day** of default, cumulatively up to **5% of the cost** of the plot/apartment/building | **Your personal exposure** | 🔴 **Very high** |

| **69** | Offences by companies | Persons in charge are deemed guilty | Relevant for non-individual agents | 🟠 Medium |
| **71** | Power to adjudicate | Adjudicating Officer decides **compensation** under s.12, 14, 18, 19 | Compensation vs interest split | 🔴 High |
| **79** | Bar of jurisdiction | **Civil courts barred** from RERA matters | Why the forum question matters | 🟠 Medium |

## 8. Case study 📋

**The pre-launch that wasn't.**

A developer offers you 2% brokerage — double the market rate — to bring buyers into a
"pre-launch" at ₹18,500/sq ft, against a post-launch price of ₹21,000. Booking amount is
₹5 lakh on a ₹2.4 crore flat. The developer says RERA registration is "filed, number expected
in three weeks." There is an allotment letter, no Agreement for Sale.

**Work through it:**

1. Is marketing this project lawful right now?
2. Which sections are being contravened, and by whom?
3. Is the ₹5 lakh booking within or outside the 10% rule?
4. What is *your* exposure if you introduce a buyer?
5. What would you tell the buyer if they insist the discount is worth it?

<details><summary>Analysis</summary>

1. **No.** s.3 bars advertising, marketing, booking, selling or offering for sale before
   registration. "Filed" is not "registered". s.5 gives the Authority 30 days, and deemed
   registration only arises if the Authority fails to act — you cannot market on the assumption.
2. The **promoter** contravenes **s.3** (penalty under **s.59** — up to 10% of estimated project
   cost). **You** contravene **s.10(a)** the moment you facilitate a sale in an unregistered project.
3. ₹5 lakh on ₹2.4 crore is ~2.08% — **within** the 10% cap, so s.13 is not breached by amount.
   But s.13 is not the live problem here; s.3 is. A booking in an unregistered project is unlawful
   regardless of how small the cheque is.
4. **s.62**: ₹10,000 **per day** for as long as the default continues, cumulatively up to **5% of
   the cost of the apartment** — on a ₹2.4 crore flat that ceiling is ₹12 lakh. Plus your
   registration is at risk. The 1% extra brokerage is ₹2.4 lakh. The maths is not close.
5. Tell them plainly: until the registration number exists, they have **no registered project,
   no model agreement, no separate bank account protection and no MahaRERA forum** if it goes
   wrong. The discount is compensation for exactly that risk. Advise waiting for the number —
   and if the developer's pricing is real, it will still be there.

**The professional point:** "pre-launch" is the most common way agents lose their registration.
The economics always look good and the legal position is never defensible.
</details>

## 9. Quiz — 10 questions

1. What is the registration threshold under s.3(2)(a)?
2. What percentage goes into the separate account, and which three professionals certify withdrawals?
3. Maximum amount a promoter may take before a registered Agreement for Sale?
4. Define carpet area under s.2(k). What is *excluded*?
5. What is the defect liability period and the rectification window?
6. Under s.12, what is the remedy if an allottee relied on a false advertisement?
7. What is the penalty on an agent under s.62 — rate and ceiling?
8. Within how many days must an appeal be filed to the Appellate Tribunal, and what must a promoter deposit?
9. What proportion of allottees must consent to alterations in sanctioned plans beyond minor additions?
10. Which section bars civil courts from entertaining RERA matters?

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. Land **exceeding 500 sq m** *or* **more than 8 apartments**, inclusive of all phases. Below both, no registration required.
2. **70%**, into a separate scheduled-bank account; certified by **Architect (Form 1), Engineer (Form 2), Chartered Accountant (Form 3)**.
3. **10%** of the cost — s.13(1).
4. Net usable floor area. **Excludes** external walls, service shafts, exclusive balcony/verandah, exclusive open terrace. **Includes** internal partition walls.
5. **5 years** from date of possession; rectify within **30 days** — s.14(3).
6. Allottee may **withdraw from the project** and get a **full refund with interest**, plus compensation — s.12.
7. **₹10,000 per day** of continuing default, cumulatively up to **5% of the cost** of the plot/apartment/building.
8. **60 days** — s.43(5); promoter must pre-deposit **at least 30%** of the penalty or the amount payable.
9. **Two-thirds** of allottees — s.14(2).
10. **Section 79.**
</details>
