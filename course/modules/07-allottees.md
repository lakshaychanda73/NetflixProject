# Module 7 — Allottees and Their Responsibilities

> **Official syllabus:** MATP Module 7 — 7.1 Rights · 7.2 Obligations · 7.3 Dispute Resolution (7.3.1 Complaints · 7.3.2 Adjudication · 7.3.3 Conciliation · 7.3.4 Appeals) · 7.4 Penal Provisions (7.4.1 Promoters · 7.4.2 Agents · 7.4.3 Allottees · 7.4.4 Offence by companies).
> **Exam weight:** 🔴 High — the penal provisions sub-module is heavily examined.
> **Time:** 4–5 hours.

---

## 1. What I need to learn

What your client is entitled to, what your client owes, and exactly what happens when it goes wrong.

Most agents know rights and forget obligations. The obligations matter: an allottee who stops
paying because construction is slow can find *themselves* in default. Advising a client badly here
costs them real money.

## 2. Simple explanation

### 2.1 Rights of allottees (syllabus 7.1) ⚖️ s.19

| s.19 | Right |
|---|---|
| **(1)** | Obtain **information** on sanctioned plans, layout plans and specifications approved by the competent authority |
| **(2)** | Know the **stage-wise time schedule** of completion, including water, sanitation, electricity |
| **(3)** | Claim **possession** of the apartment/plot as per the declaration under s.4(2)(l)(C); and the association is entitled to possession of common areas |
| **(4)** | Claim **refund with interest and compensation** where the promoter fails to comply or is unable to give possession per the agreement — under s.18 |
| **(5)** | Have **documents and plans, including common areas**, after possession |

**And through s.18:**
- If the promoter fails to complete or give possession by the agreed date, the allottee may
  **withdraw** and get a **full refund with interest** plus compensation; or **stay** and get
  **interest for every month of delay** until possession.
- If the promoter's title is defective and the allottee suffers loss, compensation is payable and
  **is not barred by limitation** under the Act's scheme.
- On any **structural defect or defect in workmanship, quality or provision of services** notified
  within **5 years** of possession, rectification within **30 days**, failing which compensation (s.14(3)).
- On **false advertisement** relied upon, withdrawal with **full refund + interest** (s.12).

### 2.2 Obligations of allottees (syllabus 7.2) ⚖️ s.19(6)–(11)

| s.19 | Obligation |
|---|---|
| **(6)** | Make **necessary payments** in the manner and within the time specified in the agreement — including registration charges, municipal taxes, water and electricity charges, maintenance charges, ground rent and other charges |
| **(7)** | Be **liable to pay interest** at the prescribed rate for any delay in payment |
| **(8)** | The obligations and liability for interest may be **reduced by mutual consent** of promoter and allottee |
| **(9)** | **Participate in the formation** of an association/society/co-operative society |
| **(10)** | Take **physical possession within 2 months** of the **Occupancy Certificate** being issued |
| **(11)** | **Participate in registration of the conveyance deed** |

> 🔴 **Exam favourite:** interest is **symmetrical**. The promoter pays the allottee for delayed
> possession; the allottee pays the promoter for delayed payment — **at the same prescribed rate**
> under the Maharashtra Rules. Any question that implies only the promoter pays interest is wrong.

> 🔴 **Second favourite:** **2 months from OC** to take possession — s.19(10). Not 30 days, not
> 3 months.

### 2.3 Dispute resolution (syllabus 7.3)

**The ladder:**

```
CONCILIATION (voluntary, fast, no adjudication)
      │
      ▼
COMPLAINT TO THE AUTHORITY ── s.31 ── (orders, directions, interest, penalty)
      │
      ├──► ADJUDICATING OFFICER ── s.71 ── (COMPENSATION only, under ss.12, 14, 18, 19)
      │
      ▼
REAL ESTATE APPELLATE TRIBUNAL ── s.43 ── (60 days; promoter pre-deposits ≥30%)
      │
      ▼
HIGH COURT ── s.58 ── (question of law only)
```

⚖️ **s.79 — civil courts are barred** from entertaining any suit in respect of matters the
Authority or Tribunal is empowered to determine.

---

#### 7.3.1 Complaints ⚖️ s.31

- **Who:** any **aggrieved person** — an allottee, an association, a promoter, or an agent.
- **Against whom:** a promoter, an allottee **or an agent** — note that agents can be complained against.
- **Where:** the Authority, or the Adjudicating Officer.
- **How (Maharashtra):** create an account on the MahaRERA complaint portal and file online.
  Start at **[Make a Complaint](https://maharera.maharashtra.gov.in/make-complaint)** and
  **[Complaint filing under a registered project](https://maharera.maharashtra.gov.in/complaint-filing-under-registered-project)**.
- ⏱ Fee is prescribed by the Maharashtra Rules — **verify the current amount on the portal**.

#### 7.3.2 Adjudication ⚖️ s.71

The **Adjudicating Officer** — a judicial officer appointed by the Authority in consultation with
the State Government — decides **compensation** claims under **ss.12, 14, 18 and 19**.

**The split you must get right:**

| Forum | Awards |
|---|---|
| **The Authority** | Orders, directions, **interest**, penalty, refund |
| **The Adjudicating Officer** | **Compensation** |

**s.72 — factors the Adjudicating Officer considers:** the amount of disproportionate gain or
unfair advantage made as a result of the default; the amount of loss caused as a result of the
default; the repetitive nature of the default; and such other factors as may be necessary.

#### 7.3.3 Conciliation ⚖️/💼

MahaRERA operates a **Conciliation Forum** — a voluntary, without-prejudice settlement mechanism
before or alongside adjudication.

- Both parties must **agree** to conciliate. Either may decline.
- Conducted by a **conciliation bench**, typically drawn from a panel with representation of both
  promoter and allottee sides ([List of Conciliators](https://maharera.maharashtra.gov.in/list-conciliators)).
- The conciliator **does not decide** — they help the parties settle. A settlement is recorded.
- ⏱ Runs on tight timelines: notice within roughly a week, first hearing within about a fortnight,
  and matters referred back to MahaRERA if settlement isn't emerging within roughly sixty days.
  **Verify current timelines at [Procedure of Conciliation](https://maharera.maharashtra.gov.in/procedure-conciliation).**
- On the first date of hearing of a complaint, if both parties agree to settle, the matter can be
  moved to the Conciliation Forum.

💼 **Advise clients honestly:** conciliation is fast and preserves the relationship, and it is
often the right first move where the promoter is solvent and the dispute is about timing or
snagging. It is the wrong move where the promoter has no money — there, you want an order.

#### 7.3.4 Appeals ⚖️ ss.43–44, 58

- Appeal from an order of the **Authority or Adjudicating Officer** lies to the **Real Estate
  Appellate Tribunal**.
- **Time limit: 60 days** from receipt of the order — **s.43(5)**.
- 🔴 **A promoter appealing must first deposit at least 30%** of the penalty, or such higher amount
  as may be determined, **or** the total amount payable to the allottee including interest and
  compensation, **whichever is higher**.
- The Tribunal endeavours to dispose of appeals within **60 days**.
- From the Tribunal, appeal lies to the **High Court** within **60 days**, on a **question of law**
  — s.58.

### 2.4 Penal provisions (syllabus 7.4)

#### 7.4.1 Penal provisions for promoters

| Section | Default | Penalty |
|---|---|---|
| **59(1)** | Non-registration under s.3 | Up to **10%** of the estimated cost of the project |
| **59(2)** | **Continued** violation after 59(1) | **Imprisonment up to 3 years** and/or further fine up to **10%** |
| **60** | Contravention of **s.4** (false information in the application) | Up to **5%** of the estimated cost |
| **61** | Contravention of **any other provision** | Up to **5%** of the estimated cost |
| **63** | Failure to comply with an **Authority** order | Daily penalty, cumulatively up to **5%** of the estimated cost |
| **64** | Failure to comply with an **Appellate Tribunal** order | **Imprisonment up to 3 years** and/or fine, cumulatively up to **10%** of the estimated cost |

#### 7.4.2 Penal provisions for real estate agents

| Section | Default | Penalty |
|---|---|---|
| **62** | Contravention of **s.9 or s.10** | **₹10,000 per day** of continuing default, cumulatively up to **5% of the cost** of the plot/apartment/building |
| **65** | Failure to comply with an **Authority** order | Daily penalty, cumulatively up to **5%** of the estimated cost |
| **66** | Failure to comply with an **Appellate Tribunal** order | **Imprisonment up to 1 year** and/or fine, cumulatively up to **10%** of the estimated cost |

#### 7.4.3 Penal provisions for allottees

| Section | Default | Penalty |
|---|---|---|
| **67** | Failure to comply with an **Authority** order | Daily penalty, cumulatively up to **5%** of the cost of the plot/apartment/building |
| **68** | Failure to comply with an **Appellate Tribunal** order | **Imprisonment up to 1 year** and/or fine, cumulatively up to **10%** of the cost |

> Note the pattern: **promoters face up to 3 years' imprisonment; agents and allottees up to 1 year.**
> Fine ceilings for disobeying a Tribunal order are 10%; for disobeying the Authority, 5%. This
> symmetry makes the whole of Chapter VIII easy to remember.

#### 7.4.4 Offences by companies ⚖️ s.69

Where an offence is committed by a company, **every person who at the time of the offence was in
charge of, and responsible to, the company for the conduct of its business**, as well as the
company itself, is **deemed guilty**.

**Defences:** the person proves the offence was committed **without their knowledge** or that they
**exercised all due diligence** to prevent it.

**Extension:** where the offence is proved to have been committed with the **consent or connivance
of, or is attributable to any neglect on the part of**, any **director, manager, secretary or other
officer**, that person is also deemed guilty.

> **Why an agent cares:** if you are registered as a **non-individual** agent, s.69 reaches the
> individuals running it. Incorporating does not put a wall between you and RERA liability.

**s.70 — compounding.** Offences punishable with imprisonment may be **compounded** by the court,
on payment of such sum as prescribed. This is how many matters actually end.

## 3. 🔴 Realistic scenarios

### Scenario A — the 18-month delay

> Your client booked a 2BHK in a Mumbai project in 2022. The registered Agreement for Sale states
> possession by **December 2024**. It is now **June 2026** — **18 months** past the agreed date.
> Your client has paid **₹1.4 crore** of a ₹1.75 crore consideration, and is paying **₹98,000/month**
> EMI while also paying **₹55,000/month** rent. The promoter has taken one extension and blames a
> delayed environmental clearance.

**Q1. What rights does the buyer have?**

Under **s.18(1)**, on the promoter's failure to give possession by the date specified in the
agreement, the allottee has **two mutually exclusive choices**:

- **Withdraw:** return of the **entire amount paid (₹1.4 crore)** with **interest at the prescribed
  rate** from the date of payment, **plus compensation** under s.71; or
- **Stay:** **interest for every month of delay** until handover.

The rate is prescribed by the **Maharashtra Rules** (commonly expressed as SBI's highest marginal
cost of lending rate plus a margin — ⏱ **verify the current rate in the Rules**, it moves with SBI's MCLR).

**Is "delayed environmental clearance" a defence?** Generally **no**. An extension under s.6 is for
**force majeure** — calamity. Approval delays are ordinarily a promoter's own project risk, and
the agreed possession date in the registered agreement is what s.18 measures against. The promoter
would have to make out a genuine force majeure case.

**Q2. What should the agent tell the buyer?**

1. **Do the arithmetic before choosing.** Withdrawal returns capital plus interest but loses the
   asset — and if Mumbai prices have moved, ₹1.4 crore + interest may not buy the same flat today.
   Staying preserves the asset and accrues monthly interest but continues the rent + EMI drain.
   **This is a financial decision, not just a legal one.**
2. **Do not stop paying.** Withholding instalments puts the client in default under s.19(6)–(7) and
   hands the promoter a counter-argument. If they want to stop, that decision should follow legal
   advice, not precede it.
3. **Interest starts from the agreed date**, not from when they complain. Delay in filing doesn't
   forfeit accrued interest, but it does delay recovery.
4. **Consider conciliation first** if the promoter is solvent and building — it is faster.
   Go straight to a **s.31 complaint** if the site is dead.
5. **Be honest about enforcement.** An order is only as good as the promoter's ability to pay.
   Recovery is as **arrears of land revenue** (s.40), which is real but slow.

**Q3. What documents are needed?**

- Registered **Agreement for Sale** (the possession date is the anchor)
- **Allotment letter**
- **All payment receipts** and bank statements
- **Home loan sanction and statement** (to evidence EMI outflow)
- **Rent agreement and receipts** (for compensation)
- The project's **MahaRERA page**, QPR history and extension record — download and date-stamp it
- All **correspondence** with the promoter
- Any **advertisement or brochure** relied on (relevant if s.12 is in play)

**Q4. What is the dispute-resolution process?**

1. Written notice to the promoter recording the delay and the election (withdraw or stay).
2. Optionally, **conciliation** via the MahaRERA Conciliation Forum.
3. **Complaint under s.31** to MahaRERA, online.
4. Authority orders refund/interest/directions; **compensation** goes to the **Adjudicating
   Officer** under s.71.
5. Either party may appeal to the **Appellate Tribunal within 60 days** — the promoter must
   **pre-deposit ≥30%** or the full amount payable, whichever is higher.
6. **High Court** on a question of law within 60 days.
7. Non-compliance by the promoter → **s.63** penalties; recovery as **arrears of land revenue** (s.40).

### Scenario B — the buyer who stopped paying

> Your client's project is 9 months behind. Frustrated, they stopped paying the last two
> construction-linked instalments — ₹22 lakh. The promoter has issued a notice threatening
> cancellation and forfeiture.

**The uncomfortable answer:** the client is now in default under **s.19(6)**, and is liable for
**interest at the prescribed rate** under **s.19(7)**. Delay by the promoter does **not**
automatically suspend the allottee's payment obligation — the Act gives the allottee a *remedy*
(s.18), not a *self-help right to withhold*.

**Better advice, in order:** pay under protest while recording the delay in writing; claim s.18
interest for the promoter's delay separately; and if cancellation is threatened, note that
forfeiture terms are constrained — the **model allotment letter caps the deduction on cancellation
at 2%** (Module 9). Never advise a client to stop paying as a negotiating tactic.

---

### Scenario C — the defect that appeared in year four

> Three years and eight months after possession, seepage appears through an external wall in your
> client's flat. The promoter says the warranty was one year.

**s.14(3)** governs, not the promoter's warranty. Any **structural defect or defect in workmanship,
quality or provision of services** brought to the promoter's notice **within 5 years** of possession
must be **rectified within 30 days**, free of charge. Failing that, the allottee is entitled to
**compensation**.

**Action:** written notice to the promoter, dated, with photographs; keep proof of delivery
(the 5-year clock and the 30-day clock both depend on that date). If unrectified in 30 days,
complain under **s.31**; compensation via **s.71**.

**three decided s.18 delay complaints** end to end. For each, note:

1. What was the agreed possession date, and how long was the delay?
2. Did the allottee elect to withdraw or to stay?
3. What did the Authority order — refund? interest? at what rate?

5. What argument did the promoter run, and did it work?

Then write a **one-paragraph summary** of what MahaRERA appears to actually do in delay cases. That
paragraph will be worth more to a client than any brochure.

## 8. Case study 📋 — the OC that arrived quietly

> Your client's project receives its **Occupancy Certificate on 3 March**. The promoter emails a
> possession notice to the address on file — an old email your client no longer checks. On
> **20 June**, the promoter demands **maintenance charges and municipal taxes from 3 March**, plus
> **interest** on delayed payment of the final instalment, and points to **s.19(10)**.

**Questions:** Is the promoter right? What is the client's exposure? What should have happened?

<details><summary>Analysis</summary>

**The promoter is substantially right on the framework.** Under **s.19(10)** the allottee must take
physical possession **within 2 months of the OC being issued** — here, by 3 May. Under **s.19(6)**
the allottee is liable for municipal taxes, maintenance and other outgoings as specified in the
agreement, and under **s.19(7)** interest runs on delayed payments. Once a valid possession offer
is made following OC, liability for outgoings typically shifts to the allottee — this is standard
and is usually spelled out in the Agreement for Sale.

**But three things are contestable:**
1. **Was the possession offer validly served?** A notice to a stale email may not be effective
   service. What does the **agreement** say about the mode of notice? That clause decides this.
2. **Was the OC valid for this unit?** A **part-OC** not covering the client's wing or floor is not
   an OC for this purpose.
3. **Was the flat actually ready to be possessed?** An offer of possession of a unit with
   incomplete work or without services is arguably not a valid offer at all.

**Client exposure:** roughly 3.5 months of maintenance and municipal taxes, plus interest on the
final instalment — real money, and largely avoidable.

**What should have happened — and this is the agent's job:** after the Agreement for Sale, you
should have (a) ensured the promoter had **current contact details**, (b) told the client the
**2-month clock** exists, and (c) set your own reminder to watch for the OC. This is exactly the
"After the transaction" section of the Module 5 compliance checklist. A five-minute follow-up would
have saved the client a few lakh rupees — and it is the kind of thing that generates referrals for
the next decade.
</details>

## 9. Quiz — 10 questions

1. Name the five rights under s.19(1)–(5).
2. Within how long must an allottee take possession after OC?
3. Does interest run only against the promoter? Explain.
4. What are the allottee's two options under s.18 for delayed possession?
5. What is the difference between what the Authority awards and what the Adjudicating Officer awards?
6. What must a **promoter** deposit before appealing, and within what time?
7. State the penalty on an **allottee** for disobeying an Authority order; a Tribunal order.
8. What is the maximum imprisonment for a promoter under s.59(2)? For an agent under s.66?
9. Under s.69, who is deemed guilty when a company commits an offence, and what are the defences?
10. Which section bars civil courts, and which section allows compounding?

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. Information on sanctioned/layout plans and specifications; stage-wise time schedule incl. water, sanitation, electricity; possession (and association's possession of common areas); refund with interest and compensation under s.18; documents and plans including common areas after possession.
2. **2 months** from issue of the Occupancy Certificate — s.19(10).
3. **No.** Interest is symmetrical — promoter pays for delayed possession (s.18), allottee pays for delayed payment (s.19(7)), **at the same prescribed rate**.
4. **Withdraw** — full refund with interest plus compensation; or **stay** — interest for every month of delay until possession.
5. Authority: orders, directions, **interest**, penalty, refund. Adjudicating Officer (s.71): **compensation** under ss.12, 14, 18, 19.
6. **At least 30%** of the penalty, or the total amount payable to the allottee including interest and compensation, **whichever is higher**; within **60 days** — s.43(5).
7. **s.67** — daily penalty up to **5%** of the cost (Authority). **s.68** — **imprisonment up to 1 year** and/or fine up to **10%** (Tribunal).
8. Promoter s.59(2): **3 years**. Agent s.66: **1 year**.
9. Every person in charge of and responsible to the company for the conduct of its business, **and the company itself**. Defences: the offence was committed **without their knowledge**, or they exercised **all due diligence** to prevent it. Directors/managers/secretaries are also liable where there is consent, connivance or neglect.
10. **s.79** bars civil courts; **s.70** allows compounding of offences punishable with imprisonment.
</details>

## 12. One-page revision sheet

> **Rights (s.19(1)-(5)):** plans · schedule · possession · refund+interest+compensation · documents after possession.
> **Obligations (s.19(6)-(11)):** pay on time · **interest if late** · form the association ·
> **possession within 2 months of OC** · register the conveyance.
>
> **Interest runs BOTH WAYS at the same prescribed rate.**
>
> **s.18 delay → allottee elects: WITHDRAW** (full refund + interest + compensation) **or STAY**
> (monthly interest until possession).
>
> **Ladder:** Conciliation → Authority (**s.31**) → Adjudicating Officer (**s.71**, compensation only)
> → Tribunal (**s.43**, **60 days**, promoter deposits **≥30%**) → High Court (**s.58**, law only).
> **s.79** — civil courts barred.
>
> **Penalties — memorise the pattern:**
> | | Non-registration | Other | Disobey Authority | Disobey Tribunal |
> |---|---|---|---|---|
> | **Promoter** | s.59: 10% (+3 yrs if continued) | s.60/61: 5% | s.63: 5% | s.64: **3 yrs** / 10% |
> | **Agent** | **s.62: ₹10k/day, 5% cap** | — | s.65: 5% | s.66: **1 yr** / 10% |
> | **Allottee** | — | — | s.67: 5% | s.68: **1 yr** / 10% |
>
> **s.69** companies — persons in charge deemed guilty; defences: no knowledge / due diligence.
> **s.70** compounding. **s.14(3)** defects: **5 years / 30 days**.
