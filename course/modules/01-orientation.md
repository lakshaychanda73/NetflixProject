and understand every word of it.

## 2. Simple explanation

### 2.1 What is real estate?

Real estate is **land plus everything permanently attached to it**, plus the *bundle of legal
rights* that come with it. That second half matters more than the first. In India you almost
never buy "a flat" — you buy a set of rights: the right to occupy, to use, to transfer, to
mortgage, to exclude others. A flat with a disputed title is bricks you cannot sell.

An agent who thinks of property as bricks gives bad advice. An agent who thinks of property
as **a rights package with a paper trail** gives good advice. That single mental shift is the
point of this whole course.

### 2.2 The ecosystem — who does what

| Player | What they actually do | Where you meet them |
|---|---|---|
| **Landowner** | Owns the land. May sell outright or contribute land to a JDA. | Start of the chain |
| **Developer** | Builds. Commercial/common term. | Everywhere |
| **Promoter** ⚖️ | **Legal term under RERA s.2(zk).** The person who constructs/converts/develops and sells. Includes development authorities and, importantly, a landowner who has entered into a JDA. Wider than "developer". | The Act, agreements |
| **Broker / Agent** ⚖️ | RERA s.2(zm): a person who negotiates or acts on behalf of one person in a transfer of a plot/apartment/building and receives fees/commission. **This is you.** | Between buyer and promoter |
| **Channel Partner** 💼 | Industry term for an agent/firm formally empanelled by a developer to sell inventory, usually on a defined commission grid. Not a legal category — a channel partner still needs agent registration. | Developer sales meetings |
| **Allottee** ⚖️ | RERA s.2(d): the person to whom a plot/apartment is allotted/sold. Includes a subsequent purchaser. **Does not include** someone to whom premises are given on rent. | Your client |
| **Investor** | Buys for yield or appreciation rather than use. Different risk appetite and exit horizon from an end-user. | Resale/pre-launch |
| **Architect** | Designs, and under RERA signs **Form 1** certifying construction stage for fund withdrawal. | Approvals, QPR |
| **Structural Engineer** | Structural design; signs **Form 2**. | QPR |
| **Chartered Accountant** | Certifies cost incurred; signs **Form 3**. | QPR |
| **Contractor** | Executes construction. Civil, MEP, finishing. | Site |
| **PMC** | Project Management Consultant — manages cost, quality, schedule on the developer's behalf. | Site, developer meetings |
| **Lawyer / Title consultant** | Issues the **Title Certificate / Search Report** that everything else rests on. | Due diligence |
| **Lender** | Bank/NBFC/HFC. Two roles: construction finance to the developer, home loan to your buyer. | Both sides |
| **Government authorities** | Planning (MCGM/BMC, MMRDA, CIDCO, SRA), registration (IGR), regulator (MahaRERA), tax (GST, Income Tax). | Every stage |

> **Agent's note:** the difference between *developer* and *promoter* is not pedantry. When a
> landowner does a JDA and the buyer sues for delay, the **landowner can be a promoter too** and
> jointly liable. Knowing who the promoter legally is tells your client who they can actually sue.

### 2.3 Market types

**Primary market** — buying from the promoter. First sale. GST may apply, RERA project
registration applies, Agreement for Sale is the instrument.

**Secondary market (resale)** — buying from an existing allottee/owner. No GST. RERA project
registration may have lapsed or completed. Instrument is usually a Sale Deed or a transfer of
the earlier agreement. Title chain matters far more here because you inherit every past defect.

### 2.4 Asset classes

| Class | Driver of value | Typical Mumbai note |
|---|---|---|
| **Residential** | Household income, interest rates, end-user demand | Largest by volume; MMR is India's biggest market |
| **Commercial (office)** | Corporate leasing, IT/BFSI/GCC demand, rent per sq ft | BKC, Lower Parel, Andheri, Goregaon, Powai |
| **Retail** | Footfall, catchment income, trade mix | High-street vs mall; revenue-share leases common |
| **Industrial / Warehousing** | Logistics, consumption, highway/port access | Bhiwandi, Panvel, JNPT corridor |
| **Land** | Zoning, FSI, infrastructure timing | Highest risk, highest leverage on approvals |
| **Redevelopment** | Existing occupants + incentive FSI | Mumbai's dominant supply engine — see Module 11 |

### 2.5 The lifecycle — land to possession

```
LAND ──► PLANNING ──► APPROVALS ──► DEVELOPMENT ──► CONSTRUCTION
                                                          │
POSSESSION ◄── REGISTRATION ◄── AGREEMENT ◄── SALES ◄── MARKETING
```

| Stage | What happens | Key document produced | Agent's risk radar |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Land** | Purchase or JDA; title search | Sale Deed / **JDA**, Title Certificate, 7/12 or Property Card | Unclear title, litigation, tenants |
| **Planning** | Architect designs to zoning + FSI | Concept plan, FSI statement | Design assumes FSI not yet sanctioned |
| **Approvals** | Plan sanction, IOD, NOCs (fire, environment, aviation, traffic) | **IOD**, sanctioned plans | Approvals "in process" at launch |
| **Development** | Site enabling, plinth | **Commencement Certificate (CC)** | Selling before CC |
| **Construction** | Structure → finishes | QPR, Forms 1/2/3 | Slipping QPR, stalled works |
| **Marketing** | Branding, pricing, channel partners | Advertisements (must carry MahaRERA no. + QR ⚖️) | Ads without registration number |
| **Sales** | Booking, allotment | **Allotment Letter** | Taking >10% without Agreement ⚖️ |
| **Agreement** | Agreement for Sale executed | **AFS** (Annexure A model form) | Deviations from model form |
| **Registration** | Stamped & registered at Sub-Registrar | Registered AFS + Index II | Unregistered agreement = weak rights |
| **Possession** | Handover after OC | **Occupancy Certificate (OC)**, possession letter | Possession offered without OC 🚩 |

Two hard legal anchors in that chain, worth memorising now because they recur in every module:

- ⚖️ **You cannot advertise, market, book or sell** in a project that requires registration until it **is** registered — RERA s.3.
- ⚖️ **The promoter cannot accept more than 10% of the cost** of the apartment as advance/application fee **without** first entering into a registered Agreement for Sale — RERA s.13(1).

## 7. Practical exercise ✍️

**Map the Mumbai ecosystem (60 minutes).**

Pick any one under-construction project you can see from a road you know. Then build a one-page map:

1. Who is the **promoter** (legal name, not brand name)?
2. Which **authority** sanctions it — MCGM, MMRDA, CIDCO, SRA?
3. Which **asset class** and which **micro-market**?
4. Is it a greenfield project or a **redevelopment**? If redevelopment, whose land was it?
5. Which lifecycle **stage** is it in right now, and what document would prove that?
6. List the **ten roles** from §2.2 and name or mark "unknown" for each.

Keep it. You'll redo it properly with portal data in Module 4 and it should look very different.

## 8. Case study 📋

**The two-brand problem.**

A buyer tells you: *"I'm buying a Lodha flat in Thane."* You look it up and the promoter on the
MahaRERA registration is a special purpose vehicle with a completely different name, and the land
is owned by a third entity that entered a JDA.

**Questions to sit with:**
- Who does the buyer have a contract with?
- If the project is delayed 2 years, who does the buyer file against under s.31?
- Does the parent brand's balance sheet protect the buyer?

**The point:** in Indian real estate the **brand you see is rarely the legal entity you contract
with**. Projects are routinely built in SPVs. This is normal and lawful — but it means brand
reputation is not the same as counterparty strength, and your client should know the difference.
Everything in Module 8 (Due Diligence) exists because of this gap.

## 9. Quiz — 10 questions

1. Under RERA, is a landowner who contributes land under a JDA a "promoter"?
2. What is the legal difference between a *broker* and a *channel partner*?
3. Does "allottee" include a tenant?
4. Name the three professionals who sign Forms 1, 2 and 3.
5. Which certificate marks the legal *start* of construction, and which marks lawful *occupation*?
6. What is the maximum percentage a promoter may take before a registered Agreement for Sale?
7. Name three drivers of value for commercial office space that don't apply to residential.
8. In a resale transaction, does GST apply?
9. Why is a title certificate more important in secondary than primary market?
10. Give one reason the brand name on a hoarding may not be the promoter's legal name.

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. Yes — s.2(zk) is deliberately wide and can make the landowner a co-promoter, jointly liable.
2. Broker/agent is a **legal category** under s.2(zm) requiring registration. Channel partner is an **industry arrangement** (empanelment with a developer) and carries no separate legal status — a channel partner still needs agent registration.
3. No — s.2(d) expressly excludes a person given premises on rent.
4. Architect (Form 1), Structural Engineer (Form 2), Chartered Accountant (Form 3).
5. Commencement Certificate (CC) starts construction; Occupancy Certificate (OC) permits occupation.
6. 10% — s.13(1).
7. Corporate leasing demand, rent per sq ft / cap rates, grade and floor-plate efficiency, tenant credit quality (any three).
8. No — GST applies only to under-construction sale by a promoter; resale of a completed unit is a transfer of immovable property and outside GST.
9. Because in resale you inherit the entire prior chain of title; any defect anywhere in that chain travels to your buyer.
10. Projects are commonly built in SPVs or joint ventures for finance and risk-ring-fencing reasons.
</details>

## 12. One-page revision sheet

> **Real estate = land + attachments + a bundle of legal rights.** The rights are the asset.
>
> **Promoter (s.2zk)** is wider than developer — includes JDA landowners.
> **Agent (s.2zm)** = negotiates for a fee. **Allottee (s.2d)** = buyer, incl. subsequent purchaser, excl. tenant.
>
> **Lifecycle:** Land → Planning → Approvals → Development → Construction → Marketing → Sales → Agreement → Registration → Possession.
>
> **Two hard rules to never forget:**
> ⚖️ s.3 — no advertising/marketing/booking/selling before project registration.
> ⚖️ s.13(1) — no more than 10% before a **registered** Agreement for Sale.
>
> **Three certificates:** IOD (permission to build to a stage) → **CC** (start) → **OC** (occupy).
> **Three forms:** 1 Architect · 2 Engineer · 3 CA.
>
> **Brand ≠ legal entity.** Always check who the promoter actually is.
