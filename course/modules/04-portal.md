## 1. What I need to learn

How to take a project name and, in thirty minutes, know more about it than the salesperson selling it.

This is the skill that makes you worth your brokerage. Any agent can show a flat. An agent who
can open the portal in front of a client and read the disclosures out loud is a different
professional entirely.

## 2. Simple explanation — the two portals

Maharashtra runs **two** systems and confusing them wastes time:

| | **Information portal** | **Application portal** |
|---|---|---|
| URL | **https://maharera.maharashtra.gov.in/** | **https://maharerait.mahaonline.gov.in/** |
| Purpose | Public information: search projects/agents, orders, circulars, guidance | Transactions: register as agent, file applications, QPR, complaints |
| You use it for | **Due diligence** | **Your own registration and filings** |

Rule of thumb: **read** on `maharera.maharashtra.gov.in`, **do** on `maharerait.mahaonline.gov.in`.

## 3. 🔴 The 30-Minute MahaRERA Project Investigation

Work these fifteen steps in order. Keep a notepad open. By step 15 you'll have a defensible view.

---

### Step 1 — Search the project *(2 min)*

**Where to click →** Public site → search registered projects. Search by **project name**,
**MahaRERA registration number**, or **promoter name**. If you have a hoarding, scan the **QR code**.
**What to look for →** An exact match, and the correct one. Common project names repeat across the state.
**What good looks like →** One clear record, with a registration number in the standard format, in
the district and taluka you expected.
🚩 **Red flag →** No record at all; or several near-identical names by different promoters; or the
project is only findable under a name the salesperson never mentioned.
**Ask the developer →** *"Give me the exact MahaRERA registration number and the registered project name."*

---

### Step 2 — Verify registration status and validity *(2 min)*

**Where to click →** The project record header.
**What to look for →** Registration number, **status** (registered / lapsed / de-registered /
extended), and the **proposed completion date**.
**What good looks like →** Status active, completion date in the future with realistic headroom.
🚩 **Red flag →** **Lapsed** or **de-registered**; or a completion date already past.
A lapsed registration means the promoter may not legally market the project.
**Ask →** *"Is the registration currently valid, and has it ever lapsed or been extended?"*

---

### Step 3 — Check the promoter *(3 min)*

**Where to click →** Promoter details tab.
**What to look for →** Legal entity name, type (individual / partnership / LLP / company), PAN-level
identity, address, and the **list of the promoter's other registered projects**.
**What good looks like →** A promoter with several past projects that **reached completion**, and
whose brand name and legal name are reconcilable.
🚩 **Red flag →** Brand-new SPV with no track record; or a promoter whose other projects show
lapsed registrations, repeated extensions or a pile of complaints.
**Ask →** *"Which entity is the promoter, and can you list your last five completed projects with their registration numbers?"*

> This step is why Module 1 insisted brand ≠ legal entity. Use the promoter search to see the
> *portfolio*, not the project in isolation.

---

### Step 4 — Check project status and progress *(3 min)*

**Where to click →** Project details → status / progress section.
**What to look for →** Declared **percentage completion**, work stage (excavation, plinth, slabs,
finishing), and consistency with the completion date.
**What good looks like →** Progress consistent with the timeline and with what you can see on site.
🚩 **Red flag →** Progress flat across consecutive quarters; or a project 30% complete with 9 months
to the declared completion date.
**Ask →** *"Show me the last four quarterly progress filings."*

---

### Step 5 — Check the completion date and any extensions *(2 min)*

**Where to click →** Registration details; extension records.
**What to look for →** **Original** proposed completion date vs **current** date, and every extension.
**What good looks like →** Original date intact, or a single well-documented extension.
🚩 **Red flag →** Multiple extensions. Under s.6 an extension is ordinarily up to **one year** and
for **force majeure**. A pattern of extensions is a pattern of missed promises.
**Ask →** *"What was the original completion date, how many extensions have you taken, and on what grounds?"*

---

### Step 6 — Check land details *(3 min)*

**Where to click →** Land details / project details tab.
**What to look for →** Survey number / CTS number, total plot area, and the **nature of the
promoter's right** — ownership, development agreement, lease.
**What good looks like →** Land area matching the sanctioned plan, clean survey identification.
🚩 **Red flag →** The promoter holds only development rights and the **landowner is not a
co-promoter**; area mismatches between land details and plans.
**Ask →** *"Who owns the land, and if it's a JDA, is the landowner a co-promoter on the registration?"*
**Cross-check →** Take the survey/CTS number to **[IGR Maharashtra](https://igrmaharashtra.gov.in/)** for the property card and the registered land document.

---

### Step 7 — Check approvals *(3 min)*

**Where to click →** Approvals / documents section.
**What to look for →** **IOD**, **Commencement Certificate**, layout approval, and NOCs (fire,
environment, aviation, water, traffic) — with **dates and issuing authority**.
**What good looks like →** CC issued and covering the floors actually being sold.
🚩 **Red flag →** CC absent or covering fewer floors than the tower being sold; approvals described
as "applied for"; NOCs missing on a high-rise.
**Ask →** *"Give me the CC and tell me up to which floor it's valid."*
**Cross-check →** **[MCGM AutoDCR](https://autodcr.mcgm.gov.in/bpamsclient2/Login.aspx)** for Greater Mumbai proposals.

---

### Step 8 — Check sanctioned plans *(2 min)*

**Where to click →** Uploaded plans/documents.
**What to look for →** Sanctioned floor plans and layout as approved.
**What good looks like →** Sanctioned plan matches the brochure — same tower count, same floors, same unit mix.
🚩 **Red flag →** Brochure shows amenities or a tower the sanctioned plan does not.
Under **s.14** the promoter must build per the sanctioned plan; alterations beyond minor additions
need **2/3rds allottee consent**.
**Ask →** *"Show me the sanctioned plan for this tower and this floor."*

---

### Step 9 — Check building and unit details *(2 min)*

**Where to click →** Building details / apartment details.
**What to look for →** Number of buildings, floors, **unit mix**, and total units — declared vs marketed.
**What good looks like →** The flat being sold to your client exists, on the declared floor, in the declared tower.
🚩 **Red flag →** More units marketed than registered; or a "new tower" that isn't on the registration.
**Ask →** *"Is this unit within the registered inventory? Show me it in the disclosure."*

---

### Step 10 — Check carpet area *(2 min)*

**Where to click →** Apartment details — declared **carpet area**.
**What to look for →** The RERA carpet area for the specific unit type, in sq m and sq ft.
**What good looks like →** The carpet area in the disclosure **matches** the number in the cost
sheet and the draft agreement.
🚩 **Red flag →** Salesperson quoting "saleable" or "super built-up"; carpet in the agreement lower
than in the disclosure. **⚖️ Sale must be on carpet area (s.2(k))** — see Module 10 for the maths.
**Ask →** *"Confirm the RERA carpet area in writing, and show me the same number in the draft agreement."*

---

### Step 11 — Check quarterly updates (QPR) *(3 min)*

**Where to click →** Quarterly progress / project updates.
**What to look for →** Filings for **each of the last four quarters**, physical and financial progress.
Financial-quarter cycle: due by the **20th of July, October, January and April**.
**What good looks like →** Four consecutive filings, on time, showing real movement.
🚩 **Red flag →** Missing quarters, or filings that repeat identical percentages. A promoter who
won't file a form on time is telling you something about how they run a site.
**Ask →** *"Why is Q__ missing?"*

---

### Step 12 — Check litigation and complaints *(3 min)*

**Where to click →** Project's litigation disclosure **and** the complaints/orders database at
[Orders & Judgments](https://maharera.maharashtra.gov.in/orders-judgements).
**What to look for →** Pending cases disclosed by the promoter, plus complaints filed against this
project or this promoter and how they were decided.
**What good looks like →** No litigation, or minor disclosed matters unrelated to title or delivery.
🚩 **Red flag →** Title litigation; multiple s.18 delay complaints; adverse orders the promoter
hasn't complied with.
**Ask →** *"List every pending case on this land and this project, and every MahaRERA complaint against you."*

---

### Step 13 — Check encumbrances *(2 min)*

**Where to click →** Portal's encumbrance disclosure, then **cross-check at IGR**.
**What to look for →** Mortgages, charges, or lender security on the land or the project.
**What good looks like →** Either no encumbrance, or a construction-finance mortgage the lender
will release unit-by-unit (normal and disclosed).
🚩 **Red flag →** Undisclosed mortgage; or a lender charge with no NOC process explained.
**Ask →** *"Is the land mortgaged? Which lender, and what's the NOC process for my buyer's flat?"*
**Cross-check →** IGR Maharashtra search / encumbrance certificate.

---

### Step 14 — Check possession position *(2 min)*

**Where to click →** Completion status; OC where uploaded.
**What to look for →** Whether **Occupancy Certificate** has been obtained (full or part), and the
declared possession timeline.
**What good looks like →** For a ready flat, a **full OC** covering the specific wing and floor.
🚩 **Red flag →** Possession being offered with **no OC**, or only a part-OC that doesn't cover the
unit. **Never let a client take possession of a unit not covered by an OC.**
**Ask →** *"Show me the OC and confirm it covers this wing and this floor."*

---

### Step 15 — Check remaining disclosures *(2 min)*

**Where to click →** The rest of the project record.
**What to look for →** Proforma **allotment letter**, **agreement for sale** and **conveyance deed**
(s.4(2)(g)); parking disclosure; common areas and amenities; the **bank account** details;
professional team (architect, engineer, CA); any deviation sheet against the model agreement.
**What good looks like →** All three proformas uploaded; the agreement is the Annexure A model form
or has a clear deviation sheet with modifications highlighted.
🚩 **Red flag →** No proforma agreement uploaded; deviations from the model form that are not
highlighted; amenities in the brochure absent from the disclosure.
**Ask →** *"Send me the uploaded proforma agreement and the deviation sheet."*

---

### Closing the loop

After 15 steps, write **five lines**: what the project is, who the promoter is, the biggest risk,
the one document you still need, and whether you would put your own client into it.

That five-line note is your product. It is what a client is actually paying you for.

## 6. Practical exercise ✍️

**Do the real thing (30 minutes, then 30 more).**

1. Pick a **live under-construction project in Mumbai or Thane** you can see advertised.
2. Run all 15 steps. Write your findings as you go.
3. Write the five-line closing note.
4. Then repeat on a **second project by the same promoter** — and compare. Portfolio behaviour
   tells you more than any single project record.

**Deliverable:** two one-page investigation notes. Keep them; they're your first work samples.

## 7. Case study 📋

**Two towers, one registration.**

You investigate a Thane project. Steps 1–5 look clean: registered, active, completion date 18 months
out. At Step 7 the **CC covers up to the 22nd floor**. At Step 9 the disclosure lists **two towers,
A and B**. The brochure shows **three towers** and your client is being sold a **34th-floor** flat
in **Tower C**.

**Questions:** What has gone wrong? Which sections bite? What do you tell the client?

<details><summary>Analysis</summary>

**Two separate problems, and both are serious.**

**Problem 1 — Tower C.** If Tower C isn't in the registration, then either it is a separate phase
that must be **separately registered** (each phase is a standalone project under the Act), or it
isn't approved at all. Marketing it engages **s.3**, and *your* facilitating it engages **s.10(a)**
with penalty under **s.62**.

**Problem 2 — the 34th floor.** The CC covers 22 floors. Floors above a CC's coverage are not
sanctioned for construction. Selling a 34th-floor flat against a 22-floor CC means selling
something that has no present legal permission to exist. Developers do routinely obtain CC in
stages and extend it — that is normal — but **"we'll get it later" is a risk your buyer is carrying,
not a technicality.**

**What you tell the client:** don't book. Ask for (a) the registration number for Tower C, and
(b) the amended CC covering floor 34. If both arrive, re-run steps 7–9 and reconsider. If the
developer treats the questions as obstruction, that is itself the answer.

**Why this case is here:** it is built from the two most common real gaps between a brochure and a
portal record. Steps 7, 8 and 9 exist specifically to catch them.
</details>

## 8. Quiz — 10 questions

1. Which portal do you use for due diligence, and which for filing?
2. What does a "lapsed" registration mean for marketing?
3. When are quarterly progress reports due?
4. Under s.6, how long is an extension ordinarily granted for, and on what ground?
5. Which three proformas must a promoter upload under s.4(2)(g)?
6. Your client's flat is on floor 30; the CC covers 24 floors. What do you do?
7. Where do you cross-check land ownership and encumbrance outside MahaRERA?
8. What consent threshold applies to alterations in sanctioned plans?
9. What's the risk of taking possession without an OC?
10. Name three things you'd check in the *promoter's other projects*.

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. Due diligence: **maharera.maharashtra.gov.in**. Filing/transactions: **maharerait.mahaonline.gov.in**.
2. The promoter may not lawfully continue to market/sell; buyers are exposed and the project's compliance status is broken.
3. Within **20 days of each financial quarter end** — by the **20th of July, October, January and April**.
4. Ordinarily **up to one year**, for **force majeure** — s.6.
5. **Allotment letter**, **agreement for sale**, **conveyance deed**.
6. Don't proceed. Ask for the amended CC covering floor 30; treat the gap as a live risk to disclose to the client in writing.
7. **IGR Maharashtra** (property card, registered documents, encumbrance) and **MCGM AutoDCR** for building proposals in Greater Mumbai.
8. **Two-thirds of allottees** for alterations beyond minor additions — s.14(2).
9. Occupation without OC is unauthorised: exposure to municipal action, difficulty getting utilities/loans transferred, and problems at resale.
10. Completion track record, lapsed/de-registered registrations, repeated extensions, complaints and adverse orders (any three).
</details>

## 11. One-page revision sheet

> **Read on** `maharera.maharashtra.gov.in` · **File on** `maharerait.mahaonline.gov.in`
>
> **15 steps:** search → status → promoter → progress → completion date → land → approvals →
> plans → building → **carpet area** → QPR → litigation → encumbrance → possession/OC → other disclosures.
>
> **The five deal-breakers:**
> 1. No registration / lapsed registration
> 2. Unit or tower not in the registered inventory
> 3. CC doesn't cover the floor being sold
> 4. Possession offered without OC
> 5. Undisclosed title litigation or encumbrance
>
> **QPR due:** 20 July · 20 Oct · 20 Jan · 20 Apr.
> **Cross-check outside MahaRERA:** IGR (title, encumbrance) · MCGM AutoDCR (plans, OC).
>
> **Always end with the five-line note.** That note is the product.
