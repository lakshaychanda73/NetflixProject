## 1. What I need to learn

The transaction, end to end, and the three documents that carry it: **allotment letter**,
**Agreement for Sale**, **conveyance/sale deed**. Plus the money mechanics — TDS and home loans —
that decide whether a deal actually closes.

---

## 2. The sales process

```
LEAD → QUALIFICATION → SITE VISIT → NEGOTIATION → BOOKING → ALLOTMENT LETTER
   → AGREEMENT FOR SALE → REGISTRATION → PAYMENT (construction-linked) → POSSESSION
```

| Stage | What happens | Your job | ⚖️ Compliance point |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Lead** | Enquiry from an ad, portal, referral | Capture and qualify fast | Ad must carry registration no. + QR (Order 46C/2025) |
| **Qualification** | Budget, loan eligibility, timeline, end-use vs investment | **Qualify hard.** An unqualified buyer wastes months | Don't overstate what they can afford |
| **Site visit** | Show flat, sample, location | Bring the **portal record**, not just the brochure | s.10(d) — facilitate information |
| **Negotiation** | Price, floor rise, parking, payment plan, freebies | Get everything **in writing** | Oral promises bind nobody |
| **Booking** | Token/booking amount | Payment to the promoter's **collection account** only | ⚖️ **s.13 — 10% cap** without registered AFS |
| **Allotment letter** | Promoter confirms allotment | Check against MahaRERA's **model allotment letter** | Cancellation deduction capped at **2%** |
| **Agreement for Sale** | The real contract | Compare against **Annexure A** + deviation sheet | ⚖️ s.13(1) — must be **registered** |
| **Registration** | Stamped and registered at the Sub-Registrar | Ensure it actually happens | Registration Act, 1908 |
| **Payment** | Construction-linked instalments | Track against QPR | ⚖️ s.19(6)–(7) — interest on delay |
| **Possession** | After OC | Watch for OC; **2-month clock** | ⚖️ s.19(10) |

💼 **A practical note on qualification:** in Mumbai, the most common reason a deal dies is that the
buyer's loan eligibility was never checked against the **all-in** cost (Module 10), only the base
price. Do the all-in number at qualification, not at booking.

## 3. The documents

### 3.1 Allotment Letter (syllabus 9.1)

**What it is →** The promoter's written confirmation that a specific unit is allotted to a specific
buyer, on stated terms, on receipt of the booking amount. It is the **first binding document** in
most transactions.

**Who signs →** Promoter and allottee.

**When used →** At booking, before the Agreement for Sale.

**Important clauses →**
- Unit identification: tower, floor, unit number
- **RERA carpet area** — must match the disclosure
- Total consideration and the breakdown of other charges
- Payment schedule
- **Cancellation and forfeiture terms**
- Possession date
- That an Agreement for Sale will be executed and registered

🚩 **Red flags →** No carpet area stated, or "saleable area" used instead · forfeiture above the
capped percentage · a possession date different from the MahaRERA declaration · consideration that
doesn't reconcile with the cost sheet · an allotment letter used as a substitute for the Agreement
for Sale beyond the 10% threshold.

#### 3.1.1 The Model Allotment Letter ⚖️/⏱

MahaRERA **prescribed a model allotment letter** and required promoters to upload their proforma
(the s.4(2)(g) obligation). Key features:

- It sets a **minimum period within which the allottee may cancel** the booking.
- It **caps the penalty the promoter may deduct at 2%** of the consideration on cancellation by
  the allottee, or on non-payment of the balance booking amount/advance.
- The promoter may amend the model **only in the allottee's favour** — i.e. only to **increase** the
  cancellation window or **decrease** the deduction percentage. Not otherwise.
- Deviations from the model proforma must be **highlighted in a different colour** and uploaded.

> 🔴 This is a genuine, concrete, client-facing thing you know that most agents don't:
> **a buyer who cancels cannot lawfully be charged more than 2%** under the model letter. Agents
> routinely watch clients lose 10% because nobody told them.

⏱ Verify the current model and its terms at
[MahaRERA — Resources for Homebuyers](https://maharera.maharashtra.gov.in/resources-homebuyers)
and the [Orders index](https://maharera.maharashtra.gov.in/order).

### 3.2 Model Form of Agreement for Sale (syllabus 9.2) ⚖️

**Source of the obligation:** **s.13** of the Act + **Rule 10 and Annexure A** of the
**Maharashtra RERA Rules, 2017**.

**What it is →** The principal contract of sale for an under-construction unit. **Rule 10**
prescribes the model form at **Annexure A**.

**Who signs →** Promoter and allottee (and the landowner/confirming party where relevant).

**When used →** Before more than **10%** of the cost is taken — and it must be **registered**.

**How much may be changed:** the promoter **may** modify Annexure A, **provided** the agreement
remains in conformity with **s.13(2)** and the rules and regulations. But:
- Clauses that are **mandatory under the Act must be retained** in every agreement.
- Provisions **contrary to statutory conditions are not binding** on the parties.
- Deviations/modifications must be **highlighted** and uploaded with a **deviation sheet**.

**Clauses to read every time:**

| Clause | What to check |
|---|---|
| **Carpet area** | Matches the MahaRERA disclosure and the cost sheet, in sq m **and** sq ft |
| **Total consideration** | Broken out: base price, floor rise, parking, club, infrastructure, maintenance advance |
| **Payment schedule** | Genuinely **construction-linked**, not date-linked |
| **Possession date** | Matches the MahaRERA declaration under s.4(2)(l)(C) |
| **Delay / interest** | Interest payable by the promoter at the **prescribed rate** — not a token amount |
| **Allottee's default** | Interest at the same prescribed rate; and what triggers termination |
| **Defect liability** | **5 years**, rectification in **30 days** (s.14(3)) |
| **Alterations** | No change to sanctioned plans without consent; **2/3rds** for other alterations |
| **Common areas & amenities** | Specifically listed — vague "as decided by the promoter" is a flag |
| **Parking** | What exactly is allotted, and on what basis |
| **Conveyance** | Timeline for conveyance to the association (s.17) |
| **Force majeure** | Narrow and defined, or open-ended? Open-ended is a flag |
| **Dispute resolution** | Cannot oust MahaRERA — s.79 bars civil courts |

🚩 **Red flags →** Area in "saleable" terms · possession date "tentative" · the promoter's delay
interest lower than the allottee's default interest (asymmetry is contrary to the Act's scheme) ·
open-ended force majeure · unhighlighted deviations · signing an unregistered agreement while
paying more than 10%.

### 3.3 Conveyance Deed / Sale Deed

**What it is →** The instrument that **transfers title**. The Agreement for Sale creates a right
to obtain conveyance; the conveyance actually conveys.

**When used →** On completion — to the individual allottee for a plot/independent unit, and to the
**association of allottees** for the land and building under **s.17**.

**Why it matters →** Where a promoter fails to convey, Maharashtra provides for **deemed
conveyance** — a route for societies to obtain title without the promoter's cooperation. This is a
recurring Mumbai problem worth knowing exists.

## 4. TDS (syllabus 9.3) ⏱

Two different TDS provisions touch a property transaction. **Do not confuse them.**

### 4.1 TDS on purchase of an apartment — Section 194-IA

| Item | Position |
|---|---|
| Who deducts | The **buyer** |
| Rate | **1%** of the consideration |
| Threshold | Consideration **₹50 lakh or more** |
| On what | The sale consideration (and, per current practice, measured against stamp duty value) |
| Form | Challan-cum-statement **Form 26QB**; certificate **Form 16B** to the seller |
| Note | No TAN required — the buyer uses PAN |

**Worked example:** flat at ₹1.20 crore. TDS = 1% × ₹1,20,00,000 = **₹1,20,000**. The buyer pays
₹1,18,80,000 to the seller and ₹1,20,000 to the government via Form 26QB.

🚩 **The classic error:** buyers deduct 1% only on the amount *above* ₹50 lakh. Wrong — **once the
threshold is crossed, TDS applies to the whole consideration.**

### 4.2 TDS on brokerage — Section 194H

| Item | Position |
|---|---|
| Who deducts | The **payer** of the commission/brokerage |
| Rate | **2%** (reduced from 5% w.e.f. **1 October 2024**) |
| Threshold | Aggregate exceeding **₹20,000** in a financial year |
| No PAN | **20%** |
| Who must deduct | Individuals/HUFs only where the previous year's turnover exceeded ₹1 crore (business) or gross receipts exceeded ₹50 lakh (profession) |

**Worked example — your own brokerage:** you invoice a developer ₹3,00,000 + GST.
TDS u/s 194H = 2% × ₹3,00,000 = **₹6,000**. You receive ₹2,94,000 plus the GST component, and claim
the ₹6,000 as credit in your return.

> ⏱ **Time-sensitive.** Rates and thresholds change at Budget. Verify at
> [incometax.gov.in](https://www.incometax.gov.in/) before quoting.

## 5. Home and mortgage loans (syllabus 9.4) ⏱

### 5.1 The basics

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Principal** | Amount borrowed |
| **Interest** | Cost of borrowing; floating rates in India are typically linked to an external benchmark (commonly the repo rate) |
| **Tenure** | Loan period, up to ~30 years |
| **EMI** | Equated Monthly Instalment — see Module 10 for the formula |
| **LTV** | Loan-to-Value — the share of property value financed |
| **Down payment** | The buyer's own contribution |
| **Pre-EMI** | Interest-only payments during construction, before full disbursement |
| **Mortgage** | The security interest the lender takes over the property |
| **APF** | Approved Project Financing — the lender's pre-approval of a project |
| **Foreclosure** | Prepaying and closing early |

### 5.2 ⏱ RBI LTV norms

Per the RBI's **Master Circular on Housing Finance**:

| Loan value | Indicative maximum LTV |
|---|---|
| Up to **₹30 lakh** | **90%** |
| Above ₹30 lakh and up to ₹75 lakh | **80%** |
| Above ₹75 lakh | **75%** |

**Critical detail for your buyer's cash planning:** banks must **exclude stamp duty, registration
and other documentation charges from the property cost** when computing LTV — except where the cost
of the unit does not exceed **₹10 lakh**, in which case they may be included.

**What this means in practice:** on a ₹1.5 crore Mumbai flat at 75% LTV, the buyer needs **₹37.5
lakh down payment plus roughly ₹9.5 lakh of stamp duty and registration** — about **₹47 lakh in
cash**, not ₹37.5 lakh. Agents who don't explain this lose deals at the last moment.

🔗 RBI Master Circular — Housing Finance: https://www.rbi.org.in/commonman/Upload/English/Notification/PDFs/46MS250915FA.pdf

### 5.3 Documents a buyer needs

Salaried: PAN, Aadhaar, 3 months' salary slips, 6 months' bank statements, Form 16 / 2 years' ITR,
employment proof, property documents, own-contribution proof.
Self-employed: PAN, Aadhaar, 3 years' ITR with computation, audited financials, 6–12 months' bank
statements, business proof, property documents.

### 5.4 Where deals die 💼

1. **All-in cost never calculated** — buyer qualified on base price only.
2. **Project not on the lender's APF list** — and no one checked.
3. **Ready reckoner value above agreement value** — extra stamp duty appears late.
4. **Co-applicant's obligations** not counted in the FOIR.
5. **Under-construction disbursement** tranches misunderstood — buyer expects the full amount at booking.

## 9. Practical exercise ✍️

**Mark up a real agreement (2 hours).**

1. Download a promoter's **proforma Agreement for Sale** from any project's MahaRERA record.
2. Open **Annexure A** of the Maharashtra Rules alongside it.
3. Go clause by clause. Mark: **M** (matches the model), **D** (deviation), **X** (contrary to the Act).
4. For every **D**, ask: does this favour the promoter or the allottee?
5. Write the three clauses you would negotiate for a buyer.

This is the exercise that turns you from someone who *shows flats* into someone who *advises on
contracts*. Do it at least twice.

## 10. Case study 📋 — the ₹42 lakh booking

> Your client books a ₹3.1 crore flat in Andheri. The developer asks for **₹42 lakh** as "booking
> and allotment" — about **13.5%** — against an **allotment letter only**, with the Agreement for
> Sale "at the time of the 3rd slab, in about five months". The allotment letter says **10% will be
> forfeited** on cancellation.

**Questions:** What's wrong, how much, and what do you do?

<details><summary>Analysis</summary>

**Two distinct breaches.**

**① The 10% cap — s.13(1).** The promoter may not accept **more than 10%** of the cost as advance
or application fee **without first entering into a registered Agreement for Sale**. 10% of ₹3.1
crore is **₹31 lakh**. The demand of ₹42 lakh exceeds it by **₹11 lakh**. Taking that money without
a registered AFS contravenes s.13, attracting penalty under **s.61** (up to 5% of the estimated
project cost).

Note the two limbs people miss: the agreement must be **entered into** *and* **registered**. A
signed-but-unregistered agreement does not unlock the >10% position.

**② The forfeiture clause.** MahaRERA's **model allotment letter caps the deduction at 2%** on
cancellation by the allottee, and permits promoters to depart from the model **only in the
allottee's favour**. A 10% forfeiture clause is a deviation **against** the allottee. On a ₹3.1
crore flat that's the difference between **₹6.2 lakh** and **₹31 lakh** — a ₹24.8 lakh exposure your
client would otherwise never know about.

**What you do:**
1. Tell the client, **in writing**, both points with the section and the order behind each.
2. Ask the developer to either **cap the booking at ₹31 lakh**, or **execute and register the AFS now**.
3. Ask for the forfeiture clause to be brought to **2%**, and point to the uploaded proforma and
   deviation sheet on the MahaRERA record — if their uploaded proforma says 2% and the letter they
   handed your client says 10%, that discrepancy is a strong negotiating fact.
4. If they refuse both: document the advice, and let the client decide with full information.

**Your own exposure:** facilitating this without raising it engages **s.10(c)** (unfair trade
practice / misrepresentation by omission) and **s.10(d)** (failing to facilitate the information
the allottee is entitled to). The written note protects your client **and** you.

**The wider point:** the 10% rule and the 2% cap are the two most commercially valuable things in
this entire module. Between them they are worth more to a client than any negotiation on base price.
</details>

## 11. Quiz — 10 questions

1. What is the maximum a promoter may take before a registered Agreement for Sale?
2. Which Rule and which Annexure prescribe the model form of agreement in Maharashtra?
3. May a promoter modify the model agreement? Subject to what?
4. What is the cap on deduction if an allottee cancels under the model allotment letter?
5. In which direction only may a promoter amend the model allotment letter?
6. TDS on purchase of an apartment: section, rate, threshold, form?
7. If a flat is ₹52 lakh, is TDS 1% of ₹52 lakh or of ₹2 lakh?
8. TDS on brokerage: section, current rate, and the date the rate changed?
9. Indicative maximum LTV for a loan above ₹75 lakh, and is stamp duty included in "cost" for LTV?
10. What must be highlighted, and how, when a promoter's agreement deviates from the model?

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. **10%** of the cost of the apartment/plot/building — s.13(1). And the agreement must be **registered**.
2. **Rule 10** and **Annexure A** of the Maharashtra RERA Rules, 2017.
3. **Yes**, provided it conforms to **s.13(2)** and the rules/regulations; clauses mandatory under the Act must be retained, and provisions contrary to statutory conditions are not binding.
4. **2%** of the consideration.
5. Only **in the allottee's favour** — increasing the cancellation window, or decreasing the deduction percentage.
6. **Section 194-IA**, **1%**, consideration **₹50 lakh or more**, **Form 26QB** (certificate Form 16B).
7. **1% of the entire ₹52 lakh** = ₹52,000. Once the threshold is crossed, TDS applies to the whole consideration.
8. **Section 194H**, **2%**, reduced from 5% with effect from **1 October 2024**. Threshold ₹20,000 in a financial year; 20% without PAN. ⏱ verify.
9. **75%**. Stamp duty, registration and documentation charges are **excluded** from cost for LTV, except where the unit cost does not exceed ₹10 lakh.
10. The **deviations/modifications** must be highlighted **in a different colour** and uploaded with a **deviation sheet**.
</details>

## 14. One-page revision sheet

> **Chain:** Lead → Qualify → Visit → Negotiate → Booking → **Allotment Letter** →
> **Agreement for Sale (registered)** → Registration → Construction-linked payments → **Possession (OC + 2 months)**.
>
> **⚖️ s.13(1):** max **10%** before a **registered** AFS. Signed ≠ registered.
> **⚖️ Model allotment letter:** cancellation deduction capped at **2%**; promoter may deviate **only in the allottee's favour**.
> **⚖️ Rule 10 + Annexure A:** the model agreement. Deviations **highlighted in a different colour** + deviation sheet.
>
> **TDS:**
> • **194-IA** — buyer deducts **1%** where consideration **≥ ₹50 lakh**, on the **whole** amount. **Form 26QB** → **Form 16B**.
> • **194H** — **2%** on brokerage (from **1 Oct 2024**), threshold **₹20,000**/FY, **20%** without PAN.
>
> **⏱ LTV:** ≤₹30L → **90%** · ₹30–75L → **80%** · >₹75L → **75%**.
> **Stamp duty & registration are EXCLUDED from cost for LTV** (unless unit ≤ ₹10 lakh).
> So cash needed = down payment **+ stamp duty + registration**. Say this at qualification, not at booking.
>
> **Always check in the agreement:** carpet area · possession date · **symmetrical** interest ·
> defect liability 5 yrs/30 days · parking · amenities · force majeure width · conveyance timeline.
