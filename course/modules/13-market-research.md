## 1. What I need to learn

How a real estate professional builds a view of a market from public data — and how to say what
you know, what you infer and what you're guessing, without blurring the three.

## 2. The twelve variables

| # | Variable | What it tells you | Where to get it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Supply** — new launches | How much is coming | Knight Frank, ANAROCK, JLL quarterly |
| 2 | **Demand** — sales/absorption | Whether it's being taken up | Same reports |
| 3 | **Inventory** — unsold stock | The overhang | Same reports |
| 4 | **Absorption rate** | Sold ÷ available | Derived |
| 5 | **Quarters-to-sell** | Inventory ÷ quarterly sales | Reports, or derived |
| 6 | **Price** | Capital values, ₹/sq ft | RBI HPI, IGR ASR, live listings |
| 7 | **Rental yield** | Income return | Live rental listings ÷ capital values |
| 8 | **Registrations** | Actual transactions, not listings | IGR Maharashtra / state registration data |
| 9 | **Infrastructure** | Future accessibility | MMRDA, MCGM, MSRDC, metro authorities |
| 10 | **Employment** | Who will live/work here | Office absorption (JLL/Colliers/CBRE), industry news |
| 11 | **Population / households** | Structural demand | Census, municipal data |
| 12 | **Interest rates** | Affordability and sentiment | RBI policy statements |

### 2.1 The four that matter most

If you only track four numbers for a micro-market:

```
LAUNCHES  ·  SALES  ·  UNSOLD INVENTORY  ·  QUARTERS-TO-SELL
```

Together they answer the only question that matters: **is supply running ahead of demand here, or
behind it?**

| Pattern | Reading |
|---|---|
| Sales > launches, QTS falling | **Tightening.** Prices have room |
| Launches > sales, QTS rising | **Loosening.** Buyers have leverage |
| Both high, QTS stable | Healthy churn |
| Both low | Illiquid — hard to exit |

⏱ At the time of writing: India's unsold inventory was **525,695 units**, up **4% YoY**, with a
quarters-to-sell ratio of **6** — and inventory increasingly **concentrated in premium and luxury**
while lower-priced housing kept absorbing. That is a segment-specific loosening, not a uniform one.
**Segment matters as much as geography.**

## 3. Reading a market report properly 🔴

**Five questions, every time:**

1. **What is the geography?** "Mumbai" in one report means MMR; in another it means Greater Mumbai
   only. Navi Mumbai and Thane may or may not be included. **Check the definition before comparing.**
2. **What is the segment?** Weighted averages across a market with luxury and affordable in it are
   nearly meaningless. Look for the segment split.
3. **Is "price" a listing price, a transaction price, or an index?** Listing prices overstate.
   RBI's HPI is built from **registration data** and is the most neutral.
4. **What's the base period?** Year-on-year against a weak base flatters everything. RBI rebased
   its HPI to **2022-23** — comparisons across the rebasing need care.
5. **Who paid for it?** Consultancy research is high quality and also marketing. It is rarely wrong,
   but it is rarely bearish either. **Triangulate.**

> 💼 **The professional habit:** never quote a single source. Take Knight Frank *and* ANAROCK *and*
> JLL on the same quarter. Where they agree, you can speak confidently. Where they diverge, that
> divergence is itself the interesting fact — and saying so is far more impressive than a confident
> wrong number.

## 5. 🎯 Practical assignment: analyse one Mumbai micro-market

**This is the capstone of the whole course.** Budget 4–6 hours.

**Pick one:** Kharghar · Ulwe · Panvel · Ghodbunder Road (Thane) · Mulund · Chembur ·
Borivali East · Goregaon East · Wadala.

### Step 1 — define the boundary (15 min)
Write down exactly what you're including. Pin nodes: which stations, which roads. **Every number
you produce afterwards must refer to this boundary.**

### Step 2 — the supply pipeline (60 min) 🏛
On **MahaRERA**, search registered projects in that taluka/village. Record: number of registered
projects, their declared completion dates, total registered units where visible, and how many are
**lapsed or de-registered**. This is your **forward supply pipeline**.

### Step 3 — demand and absorption (45 min) 🏢
From the latest **JLL / ANAROCK / Knight Frank** quarter, extract launches, sales, unsold
inventory and QTS at the closest available geography. Note where their boundary differs from yours.

### Step 4 — prices (60 min)
- **Official floor:** ready reckoner values from [e-ASR](https://igrmaharashtra.gov.in/Home/asr_about)
- **Asking prices:** sample **at least 10** live listings. Convert **every one to ₹ per carpet sq ft** (Module 10)
- **Neutral series:** the latest RBI HPI reading for Mumbai
Report a **range**, not a point. A single "average price" for a micro-market is almost always misleading.

### Step 5 — rental market (30 min)
Sample 10 rental listings for a comparable 2BHK. Compute **gross yield** against your median
capital value. Compare with the ~2.5–3.5% Mumbai residential norm.

### Step 6 — infrastructure (45 min) 🏛
List every infrastructure project affecting the boundary, with **status and expected completion**,
from MMRDA / MCGM / metro authority sources. **Distinguish announced from under construction from
commissioned** — they price completely differently.

### Step 7 — developers (30 min) 🏛
Identify the three most active promoters. For each, pull their MahaRERA track record: completed
projects, delays, extensions, complaints.

### Step 8 — write it (60 min)
**Two pages maximum:**
1. What this micro-market is, and who buys here
2. Supply pipeline
3. Demand and absorption
4. Price range on carpet, and rental yield
5. Infrastructure, with timing
6. The three main developers
7. **Your view** — and separately, **what would change your mind**
8. **Sources, with dates**

### The discipline that makes it professional
Mark every statement:
- **[F]** Fact — sourced, with a link and a date
- **[I]** Inference — reasoning from facts
- **[O]** Opinion — your judgement

Most agents produce pure **[O]** and present it as **[F]**. Doing this properly, once, will make
you noticeably better than the market.

## 6. Case study 📋 — two reports, one market, different answers

> Report A says a micro-market's prices rose **9% YoY**. Report B says **3%**. Both are reputable
> and cover the same quarter. Your client asks which is right.

**The answer is almost always: both, and the difference is definitional.** Work through it:

1. **Geography.** Does A cover a wider area including a premium pocket that pulled the average up?
2. **Segment.** Is A a weighted average across all launches while B tracks a fixed basket? If
   luxury launches dominated the quarter, a weighted average rises without any individual flat
   getting more expensive. **This is the single most common cause.**
3. **Price definition.** Listing/asking prices (A) vs registered transaction values (B)? Asking
   prices run ahead in a rising market and lag in a falling one.
4. **Base period.** Is one comparing against a COVID-depressed base?
5. **Method.** Simple average vs median vs hedonic index. Averages are dragged by outliers.

**What you say to the client:**
> *"They're measuring different things. A is a weighted average of new launches across a wider
> area, which rises when the mix shifts to premium product. B tracks registered transactions in a
> narrower basket. For your decision — you're buying a specific 2BHK in a specific building — the
> number that matters is neither: it's the registered transaction values for comparable units in
> that building over the last six months, which we can pull from IGR. Let me do that."*

**That answer is worth more than either report.** It shows you understand what the numbers are,
you're not selling a narrative, and you know where the *decision-relevant* data actually lives.

## 10. Quiz — 10 questions

1. Name the four core metrics for a micro-market.
2. Launches exceed sales and QTS is rising. What's happening?
3. Why is the RBI HPI more neutral than a consultancy price series?
4. Where would you find a forward supply pipeline that market reports don't give you?
5. Two reports disagree on price growth. Name three possible definitional causes.
6. What's the difference between listing prices and registered transaction values?
7. Why does segment mix distort a weighted average price?
8. Distinguish "announced", "under construction" and "commissioned" infrastructure for pricing.
9. What is the ready reckoner, and what is it useful for in research?
10. What do [F], [I] and [O] stand for, and why does the distinction matter?

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. **Launches, sales, unsold inventory, quarters-to-sell.**
2. Supply is running ahead of demand — the market is **loosening** and buyers have leverage.
3. It is built from **actual transaction/registration data** across ten cities, by a party with no commercial interest in the outcome.
4. **MahaRERA** — registered projects by taluka/village with declared completion dates and unit counts.
5. Geography definition; segment/mix; price definition (listing vs registered vs index); base period; averaging method (any three).
6. Listing prices are asks — they run ahead in rising markets and lag in falling ones. Registered values are what was actually paid and stamped (though they have their own floor effects from the ASR).
7. A weighted average rises when the mix shifts towards expensive product, even if no individual unit's price changed.
8. Value typically moves on **announcement**, again during **visible construction**, and least on **commissioning** — by which point it is largely priced in.
9. The **Annual Statement of Rates (ASR)** — the government's official minimum value per locality. In research it gives you an official price floor and a locality-level series independent of listings.
10. **Fact / Inference / Opinion.** Separating them is what makes a brief trustworthy — and stops you presenting a guess as data.
</details>
