Executive Summary

Read this page before the meeting. Everything after it is evidence.

60/100
Conditional — buy the land story, not the current price

A genuinely scarce 150-acre contiguous holding inside the strongest infrastructure catchment in coastal Maharashtra, offered at a price 3.7–5× the nearest comparable developed-plot benchmark, by a two-year-old LLP with no publicly visible MahaRERA registration. Five specific fixes take the score to 70/100.

Top 10 findings

  1. The price is the risk, not the land. God City lists from ₹35,000/sq yd (≈₹3,889/sq ft). Developed MOPA-side layouts in Sawantwadi trade at ₹7,000–9,500/sq yd and Goa-side land near Mopa at roughly ₹4,200–10,000/sq yd. Only one scheme in the district prices higher — and it has a golf course. Reported
  2. Its nearest competitor is bigger, dearer and further along. Yugen Golf City / Garden of Eden at Village Banda, Sawantwadi is a 500-acre master plan at ₹49,990/sq yd with a 9-hole IDG golf course, Wyndham-managed amenities, a helipad and a MahaRERA-registered plotted phase backed by ₹350 crore. Reported
  3. No MahaRERA number, village, survey number or coordinates are publicly discoverable for God City. Under Section 3 of RERA, a plotted scheme above 500 sq m and eight plots must be registered before it is marketed. This is the single largest diligence gap. Gap
  4. Two founders, a ten-year corporate trail, but no delivered real-estate project of their own. God City LLP (LLPIN ACF-3634, incorporated 6 February 2024, RoC-Kanpur, ₹1,00,000 contribution, Noida) is run by Rakesh Chaudhary and Suresh Chaudhary — recorded at MCA as Rakesh Kumar and Suresh Kumar. Both have been directors of Delhiwood Studios Pvt Ltd since 2016 and co-produced the 2017 Punjabi feature Hard Kaur; Rakesh Chaudhary claims 18+ years in real estate including a directorial role on VHR Group's Winsten Park in Greater Noida. No completed real-estate development delivered by them as principal was found. See Section 02. Verified
  5. Mopa is working. Manohar International Airport handled 5.38 million passengers in FY2025–26, up 15.8%, on 38,111 aircraft movements (+21%) against 7.7 million capacity — the demand engine for this entire micro-market is real and still has headroom. Verified
  6. The Shaktipeeth Expressway terminates in this taluka. The revised 856.765 km Pavnar–Patradevi alignment approved 1 April 2026 ends at Banda in Sawantwadi taluka, Sindhudurg, with ₹20,787 crore sanctioned for land acquisition and completion targeted 2028–29. No other catalyst matters as much to this specific location. Reported
  7. Land within 25–30 km of Mopa appreciated about 28% in 18 months across 2024–25, and Pernem on the Goa side is reported up 261.5% over three years — but that appreciation has already been priced into schemes at God City's level. Reported
  8. Environmental overhang is live, not historic. The Centre re-issued the 7th Western Ghats ESA draft on 27 July 2026 covering 17,340 sq km in Maharashtra; the Bombay High Court has directed that the Sawantwadi–Dodamarg wildlife corridor be declared an ESZ with a tree-felling embargo until notification; and the 29.53 sq km Tillari Conservation Reserve sits in Dodamarg. Reported
  9. At 150 acres the project qualifies for Integrated Township status (60.7 ha against a 40 ha threshold) — unlocking additional FSI on premium and a 50% stamp-duty concession on the first sale to each buyer. Almost none of the 11–78 acre competitors can access this. Reported
  10. “New Goa” is not an ownable position. GMR markets Mopa as “New Goa International Airport”, at least one other developer sells a “New Goa” scheme in Sindhudurg, and the phrase is generic across the corridor. The brand is currently undifferentiated. Verified

Top 10 recommendations

  1. Publish the MahaRERA registration number, village, survey numbers and layout sanction — this week. Nothing else in this list works until a buyer can verify the asset on maharera.maharashtra.gov.in. If registration is not yet obtained, stop pre-selling until it is.
  2. Reprice the standard inventory to ₹18,000–24,000/sq yd and reserve ₹28,000–35,000 for genuinely differentiated stock (view, corner, water-facing, clubhouse-adjacent). Hold the headline number by moving it onto a minority of plots rather than defending it across the whole layout.
  3. Apply for Integrated Township Project status immediately. 150 acres clears the 40 ha threshold. The 50% stamp-duty concession is worth roughly ₹2.5–3 lakh on a ₹1 crore plot — a closing tool no small competitor can match.
  4. Build one anchor amenity that Yugen cannot copy cheaply and finish it before Phase 1 closes. A completed, photographable clubhouse plus a wellness or water asset converts far better in this segment than a rendered golf course a buyer cannot see.
  5. Commission and publish an environmental and title pack: ESA/ESZ non-applicability, CRZ non-applicability (or the MCZMA position), Section 44 NA order, Form D-3 layout sanction, 7/12 extracts and a search report. Sell the file, not the brochure.
  6. Front-load Phase 1 so it self-funds the project. On our estimates, roughly 37% of Phase 1 at list price covers all Phase 1 costs; the full 22.5 acres at list price returns about ₹221 crore against ₹96 crore of cost. Do not open Phase 2 land spend before Phase 1 crosses break-even.
  7. Sell a villa, not a plot. Plots yield nothing. Offer a fixed-price build package and a managed rental programme; on our estimates a built 300 sq yd villa runs to a 5–6% net yield versus 0% on bare land, and it converts investors who will not otherwise buy dirt.
  8. Target Mumbai and Pune first, NCR second, NRI third. The sponsor's Noida base is a real distribution asset for a Delhi-NCR channel that most Konkan developers cannot reach — but Mumbai and Pune are where the second-home money for this coast actually lives.
  9. Replace “New Goa” with an ownable proposition. Lead on what is verifiable and scarce: registered title, 150 contiguous acres, 30 minutes to an international airport, and the expressway terminus. “Goa prices you out. Sindhudurg lets you in.”
  10. Publish a phase-wise price ladder and honour it. 8–12% escalation per phase, announced in advance, creates urgency honestly and protects early buyers — the single most effective retention tool in plotted sales.

Tourism, beaches and landmarks nearby

Sindhudurg is Maharashtra's first declared tourism district, holds 37 forts — the highest count of any district in the state — and has 5,207 sq km of area with a population of 849,651 that is only 12.59% urban (Census 2011). The relevant attractions for a second-home buyer within 30–90 minutes are Vengurla, Nivati, Bhogwe, Shiroda, Mochemad and Redi beaches; Tarkarli and Malvan with Sindhudurg Fort and Maharashtra's only marine sanctuary (Malvan Marine Sanctuary, 27 sq km, established 1987); Amboli hill station and waterfall; and Sawantwadi Palace with its ganjifa and lacquerware crafts.

Social infrastructure

This is the belt's genuine weakness and should be handled honestly in the sales pitch. Healthcare is the strongest element: Government Medical College Sindhudurg at Oros, Kudal (established 2021) is a tertiary teaching hospital and among the largest in the district, and SSPM Medical College & Lifetime Hospital, Kudal (2020) adds private capacity — both roughly 40–60 km north of the border belt. Beyond that, retail, schooling and restaurant infrastructure at an urban standard is thin; buyers with young children will treat this as a holiday and retirement location, not a primary residence. Panaji and Goa's private schools and hospitals are the practical fallback at 60–90 minutes.

FSI / FAR and development potential

For a plotted scheme the developer sells land, not built area — FSI accrues to the plot buyer at the time of construction, and is governed by the UDCPR 2020 as applied to Sindhudurg's non-corporation areas together with the district's regional plan. In rural and gram-panchayat settlement classes the basic residential FSI is typically at the low end (around 1.0), with premium FSI of 0.5 purchasable at 35% of the annual statement of rates land value. Buyers in this belt are routinely told “G+4 permissible” by competing schemes; that should be verified against the sanctioned layout rather than accepted.

The overlooked structural advantage

At 150 acres the project is 60.7 hectares — comfortably above the 40-hectare contiguous threshold for Integrated Township Project status in Maharashtra. That status carries additional built-up area on payment of premium and, commercially far more important, a 50% stamp-duty concession both on the developer's land purchase and on the first transaction with each buyer of a unit under the approved master layout. Competing schemes of 11, 51 and 78 acres cannot access this. It is the single strongest un-played card in the project.

About VHR Group, for context

Because the Winsten Park association is the strongest real-estate credential on offer, it is worth knowing what it refers to. VHR Group was established in 1986 in Sector 63, Noida, growing from a consultancy into a developer across residential, commercial, industrial and hospitality projects in Greater Noida, Ghaziabad, Delhi-NCR, Dehradun and Ooty, with 30+ completed projects claimed and R D Verma as a principal figure. Winsten Park is among its landmark developments. This is a substantial, long-established firm — but it is a third party. Experience gained at VHR Group is a professional credential belonging to an individual; it is not a delivery record belonging to God City LLP.

What this improves

This is materially better than an unknown promoter. There is a ten-year corporate trail under the same two names (Delhiwood Studios, 2016), a completed and released project with independent third-party coverage, a documented association with an established 1986-vintage NCR developer, and an exact reconciliation between the marketing names and two separate sets of statutory filings. The founders also bring genuine Delhi-NCR distribution reach — a channel almost no Konkan developer can access, and the reason the NCR market earns a place in the channel plan in Section 10. On this evidence the Development feasibility score is raised from 6 to 7, taking the overall score from 59 to 60/100.

What this does not change

Nothing found establishes a completed real-estate development delivered by these founders as the developer. Winsten Park belongs to VHR Group. Delhiwood Studios' verifiable delivered output is a feature film, on paid-up capital of ₹1 lakh. The Farmlands and the film city are at marketing or vision stage. On that reading, the Sindhudurg township — modelled at a ₹357 crore fixed cost base across 150 acres — would be the founders' first ground-up real-estate delivery as principal, and it is an unusually large one to start with. Both God City LLP and Delhiwood Studios carry ₹1 lakh of capital; thin holding vehicles are ordinary in Indian real estate, but they place the entire weight of buyer protection on RERA registration and escrow — which is precisely the document this project has not published.

Questions to put to the founders at the meeting

  1. Which entity signs the agreement to sell — God City LLP, or another entity? Is the land owned by that entity, or held under a development agreement or agreement to sell?
  2. What was the exact role, title and period at VHR Group on Winsten Park, and can VHR confirm it?
  3. What is the funding plan for a ₹357 crore cost base against ₹1 lakh of partner contribution — equity infusion, debt, or funded entirely from Phase 1 collections?
  4. Why is Wasim Ahamad, a co-director at Delhiwood and co-producer of the film, not a partner in God City LLP?
  5. What is the status of The Farmlands in Faridabad — how many acres sold, how many buyers, and what infrastructure has been delivered on the ground? This is the closest analogue to what is being attempted in Sindhudurg and is the single most informative reference to check.
  6. Audited financials for God City LLP and Delhiwood Studios for the last two years.

Read the supply number carefully

At an assumed 58% saleable efficiency, ~1,529 announced acres is roughly 4.3 million saleable square yards, or about 14,300 plots of 300 sq yd. Sindhudurg's entire population is 849,651. This inventory is not being built for local demand — it is a bet on Mumbai, Pune, NCR and NRI second-home buyers arriving in numbers that no one has yet demonstrated. That is the defining supply-side risk in this market, and it argues strongly for pricing to move volume early rather than holding out for a headline rate.

Rental market

Bare plots generate no rent. The rental case rests on building a villa and operating it as a short-stay let. Malvan alone lists 210+ vacation rentals, and the argument advanced by local promoters — that North Goa's short-let market is saturated at 70–90% peak occupancy while Sindhudurg offers North Goa-style returns at a fraction of the entry cost — is directionally credible. But Sindhudurg is materially more seasonal than Goa: the June–September monsoon is effectively dead for beach tourism, which caps realistic annual occupancy well below Goa's. Our modelled yield in Section 08 uses 38% annual occupancy for that reason.

Project Positioning

What God City's strongest USP actually is

Not “New Goa” — that phrase is used by GMR for the Mopa airport itself, by at least one competing developer for its own Sindhudurg scheme, and generically across the corridor. It is unownable and it invites the one comparison God City cannot win: against actual Goa.

The real USP is structural and defensible: 150 contiguous acres inside the Shaktipeeth terminus taluka, 30 minutes from a working international airport, at a scale that qualifies for Integrated Township status. Scale is what God City has that Cida De Luxora (11 acres), GoBoundry (11 acres) and Northern Hills (51 acres) do not, and township status is a legal advantage that even Yugen's phased 44–55 acre releases may not claim as cleanly.

Ideal target customer

A Mumbai or Pune professional or business owner, aged 38–55, buying a 300–400 sq yd plot at a ₹60–85 lakh ticket, intending to build within three to five years, motivated by the Goa-adjacency and the NH-66 drive time, and requiring a MahaRERA number before signing. The NRI and HNI segments pay more but convert far more slowly and demand a managed-build service God City does not yet offer — they are the Phase 2 and 3 customer, not the launch customer.

Product and amenity gaps in this market

Every competitor in the belt sells the same thing: a fenced plot with a promised clubhouse. The gaps are consistent and none of them are expensive relative to a golf course:

  • Nobody is selling yield. Plots produce nothing until built. Konkan Estates' managed mango-farm product is the only yield-bearing structure in the region and it is not in this belt. A fixed-price build package plus a managed short-let programme converts the investor segment that will not buy dirt.
  • Nobody has finished a clubhouse. Every scheme markets renders. The first developer to hand a buyer a photograph of a completed, operating clubhouse on site wins the credibility contest outright.
  • Nobody publishes a compliance pack. 7/12 extracts, search report, NA order, Form D-3 sanction, CRZ and ESA position — assembled into a downloadable file — would be unique in this market and directly targets the NRI and HNI objection.
  • Nobody offers monsoon-proofing. This coast floods. A published drainage and access design, and a guarantee of year-round road access, addresses a fear every buyer has and no brochure mentions.
  • No branded hospitality anchor below Yugen's price point. A 20–30 key boutique resort or serviced-villa operator inside the township gives buyers a rental channel and gives the developer a photographable asset at a fraction of a golf course's cost.

What God City should offer that competitors do not

A completed clubhouse before Phase 1 closes, a published compliance file, Integrated Township stamp-duty savings passed to the buyer, and a fixed-price build-plus-manage programme. Together these cost a fraction of a golf course, are deliverable inside 24 months, and attack precisely the four objections that stop this market's buyers from signing.

  • Premiums: corner +8%, water or valley view +12–15%, clubhouse-adjacent +10%, wider frontage +5%. Publish the grid so buyers see the logic.
  • Discounts: cap at 6%. Full upfront payment 5–6%; launch cohort (first 30 bookings) 4% plus a locked Phase 2 option; bulk purchase of 3+ plots 5%. Never discount below the ₹14,000 floor, and never quietly — an unpublished discount destroys the escalation ladder's credibility.
  • Pass through the township stamp-duty concession once Integrated Township status is granted. It is worth ₹2.5–3 lakh on a ₹1 crore plot and costs the developer nothing.

Investment score

Investment thesis

Why this project could succeed

The macro case is real and unusually well-evidenced. Mopa is not a promise — it moved 5.38 million passengers last year and grew 15.8% with capacity to spare. NH-66 is in its final stretch. The Shaktipeeth Expressway's revised alignment, approved in April 2026 with ₹20,787 crore committed to land acquisition, terminates in the very taluka this land sits in or beside. The state and the Centre have between them put ₹159 crore into a national-first underwater museum forty minutes away, and a daily express from Mumbai's CSMT started running in September 2026. Meanwhile Goa has priced out its own buyers, and that overflow has to go somewhere; Sindhudurg's border belt is the obvious place. Against that, 150 contiguous acres is a genuinely scarce asset — and it is large enough to claim Integrated Township status, an advantage that most of the competing schemes, at 11 to 78 acres, structurally cannot match. The modelled economics are forgiving: because plotted development carries no vertical construction cost, break-even sits near ₹9,400/sq yd and Phase 1 alone, sold at anything close to list, funds the remainder of the project.

Why this project could fail

It is priced as though all of that has already happened. At ₹35,000 per square yard the buyer pays roughly four times what comparable developed layouts in the same belt command, and is asked to do so by an LLP incorporated in February 2024 with a ₹1,00,000 contribution and a registered office 1,600 kilometres from the site. Its founders are not novices — a decade of corporate history, a released feature film, an association with an established NCR developer — but nothing they have delivered as principal is a real-estate development, and this would be a 150-acre one to begin with. Most seriously, there is no MahaRERA registration number, village name or survey number that a buyer or an investor can verify. In a market where the two largest competitors are registered, better capitalised and four times the size, that combination is not a premium position; it is an unsupported one. Layered on top is an environmental overhang that is active rather than historic: a seventh Western Ghats ESA draft issued in July 2026 whose village schedule is still contested, a High Court direction to declare the adjoining Sawantwadi–Dodamarg corridor an eco-sensitive zone with a standing tree-felling embargo, and a conservation reserve in Dodamarg. And roughly 1,529 acres of announced competing supply is being aimed at a second-home buyer pool no one has yet demonstrated exists at that volume. Plotted land throws off no cash. If absorption stalls, there is no rent to carry the asset and no secondary market to exit into — the only liquidity is the next sale.

Verdict

Conditional — 60/100 today, 70/100 achievable. For the developer: the land and the timing are right, the price and the disclosure are wrong, and both are entirely within your control. Fix registration, reprice the standard inventory, claim township status, and finish a clubhouse — and this becomes the best-positioned plotted scheme in the belt. For an investor or buyer: do not transact until the MahaRERA number, the survey numbers, the ESA/ESZ position and the developer's audited financials are in hand. With those four documents, at a price in the ₹18,000–24,000 band, this is a defensible five-to-eight-year hold. Without them, at ₹35,000, it is not an investment — it is a bet on someone else's paperwork.
